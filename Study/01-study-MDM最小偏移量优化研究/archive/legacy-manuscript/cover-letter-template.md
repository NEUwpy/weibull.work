## Key Contributions

This paper advances the offset parameter δ in the Minimum Discrepancy Method (MDM) for three-parameter Weibull distribution from an empirical setting (δ=0.1) to a principled decision problem defined by information hierarchy. We:

1. Establish a six-level information hierarchy (L1-L6) for offset selection, quantifying the precision gradient from simple look-up tables to oracle-level grouping.
2. Demonstrate that a Vector-MLP trained on 13 observable sample statistics can predict 26-dimensional risk curves and achieve sample-adaptive offset selection within a discrete parameter grid.
3. Validate the method on NIST 6061-T6 real fatigue-life data (n=101) using repeated holdout validation with one-sample two-sided KS distance as the primary metric.
4. Identify clear evidence boundaries: the NN selector does not outperform fixed δ=0.1 on this single real dataset, and continuous-space generalization remains unverified.
