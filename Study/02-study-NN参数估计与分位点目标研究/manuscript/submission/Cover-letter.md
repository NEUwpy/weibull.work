Please consider our research article, “Task alignment in three-parameter Weibull life-point estimation: benefits, costs, and parameter constraints,” for publication in [Journal].

The manuscript examines a practical distinction in small-sample reliability estimation: minimizing errors in distribution parameters need not minimize errors in the life point ultimately used. Using matched simulation samples and the same three-output neural architecture, we quantify the modest target benefit of single-point supervision, the accompanying parameter compensation, and deterioration at other reliability life points. Parameter constraints repair this deterioration, while a fixed-weight parameter penalty achieves similar accuracy. The findings identify where pooled gains arise and distinguish average-risk gains from typical errors, feasibility requirements, and training cost.

The analysis combines a controlled common-budget comparison, exact prediction-level contribution identities, regional error summaries, and supplementary controls. The paper explicitly limits its conclusions to a fixed-scale, uncensored simulation domain and distinguishes analyses on existing samples from independent confirmation. Code, paired predictions, derived tables, and figure sources have been assembled for reproducibility.
