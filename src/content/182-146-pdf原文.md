# A Knowledge Synthesis Method for Weibull Distribution Estimation with Four Right-Censored Life Data

Liyang Xie, Jungang Ren, Jiaxin Song and Ningxiang Wu

Lab. Vibration and Control of Aero-Propulsion System

Northeastern University

Shenyang, China

Abstract—In the situation of product reliability estimation that all the observations available are right-censored, three-parameter Weibull distribution is applied to describe product life. A new way is presented to estimate Weibull location parameter according to four right-censored life data. Based on the property of normal distribution, the knowledge on product life distribution, and Monte Carlo simulation result, a non-parametric estimation method is applied to estimate Weibull location parameter. By an example, it is demonstrated that using three-parameter Weibull distribution is evidently better than using two-parameter Weibull distribution.

Keywords-Three-parameter Weibull distribution; product reliability; Monte Carlo sampling; small size sample

## I. INTRODUCTION

Product reliability estimation often needs to be accomplished in condition of very limited information, such as a very small size sample containing only a few right-censored life data. Parametric estimation method is usually applied in such situations. Weibull distribution is by far the world’s most popular statistical model for life data [1]. Nevertheless, the mostly applied is two-parameter Weibull distribution [2-5].

Traditionally, three-parameter Weibull distribution was applied mainly for large size sample. In an example of modeling censored lifetime data for components, the sample sizes are 50 and 304, respectively [1]. Nagatsuka proposed a least squares procedure for parameter estimation of threeparameter Weibull distribution based on censored samples with sample sizes from 7 to 20 [6]. Another study to describe material probabilistic life by three-parameter Weibull distribution is based on 14 observations [7].

To estimate product life distribution and reliability in the situation of small size sample, Yang et al proposed a new Bayesian method using a modified Weibull distribution to describe the failure rate over entire lifetime [8]. Abbasi proposed a simple neural network that simultaneously estimates the three parameters, exploiting the concept of moment method to estimate Weibull parameters using mean, standard deviation, median, skewness and kurtosis [9].

The present paper focuses on new ways to estimate the location parameter of Weibull distributed product life in the situation of only four right-censored life data available.

## II. LOCATION PARAMETER ESTIMATION

The probability density function, cumulative distribution function and survival function (reliability function) of the three-parameter Weibull distribution are, respectively

$$
f (t) = \frac {\beta (t - \gamma) ^ {\beta - 1}}{\eta^ {\beta}} \exp [ - (\frac {t - \gamma}{\eta}) ^ {\beta} ]\tag{1}
$$

$$
F (t) = 1 - e ^ {- [ (t - \gamma) / \eta ] ^ {\beta}}, \quad t \geq \gamma\tag{2}
$$

$$
R (t) = e ^ {- [ (t - \gamma) / \eta ] ^ {\beta}}, \quad t \geq \gamma\tag{3}
$$

where, β, η and γ stand for the shape parameter, scale parameter and location parameter, respectively.

Three-parameter Weibull distribution is flexible and can be applied to fit the life distributions of a variety of products. However, owing to the difficulty to estimate the location parameter, especially in the situation of small size sample of censoring data, the location parameter is often assumed to be zero, thus simplifies the three-parameter Weibull distribution into two-parameter Weibull distribution. Evidently, assuming location parameter to be zero is unreasonable in situations that the lower bound of the life distribution is much greater than zero.

In fact, the majority of mechanical products such as bearings, gears, motors, etc., population life is a random variable scattering in a finite interval, and the minimum is usually much greater than zero, especially comparing to the mean or median of the population. Therefore, three-parameter Weibull distribution is necessary to describe the life random variable reasonably and precisely.

A typical scenario is that, for a new type of mechanical product of which life distribution and reliability estimation will be put into practice, while the only information available is a few right censored life data (n right-censored life data are denoted as (t+, n), where t+ is the suspending time). The case is only four products available, and the only information is that the lives of all the four products are greater than 3000h. To reasonably describe its life probability density function, threeparameter Weibull distribution needs to be applied, and the related parameters need to be estimated.

For the same kind of Weibull distributed mechanical product life random variables, shape parameter can be approximately taken the same value. For instance, most of the civil ball bearings have a shape parameter around 2.0.

For the situation that only some right censored life data $( \mathrm { t ^ { + } , n } )$ are available, Weibull scale parameter can be estimated by single-side interval estimation method. Given a confidence interval C, the estimator is

$$
\eta = \frac {(t ^ {+} - \gamma) n ^ {1 / \beta}}{[ - \ln (1 - C) ] ^ {1 / \beta}}\tag{4}
$$

The estimator indicates that, for a given right censored life sample, the smaller the location parameter, the larger the estimated scale parameter. The limit situation is the location parameter equaling to zero, i.e. a two-parameter Weibull distribution. If there is no enough information available, an acceptable choice is a conservative location parameter, less than the true value to avoid over estimating product reliability, but larger than zero to avoid over conservative.

In this paper, a non-parametric method is applied to estimate the Weibull location parameter together with an assumption that the lower bound of the 6 interval is the lower limit of product life. That is, when the logarithm of product life is described by a normal distribution, it can be assumed that all the possible life will be inside the 6 interval.

Generally, the logarithm of product life can be approximately described by normal distribution. Therefore, the property of normal distribution can be used to approximately estimate the lower 6 bound. Besides, since it is well known that, for the majority of the modern industrial products, the deviation coefficient of the normal-distributed log-life is usually less than 0.05.

For the situation of n random life observations available, the minimum t(1,n) (conservatively, the censoring time t<sup>+</sup> for the situation that only right-censored life data are available) corresponds a cumulative probability (1-0.3)/(n+0.4) estimated by median rank estimator. Therefore, t(1,n) can be taken as the $( ( 1 - 0 . 3 ) / ( n + 0 . 4 ) { \times } 1 0 0 ) ^ { \mathrm { t h } }$ percentile in statistic sense. Particularly, when $\mathrm { n } { = } 4 .$ , the cumulative probability equals to 0.159, that is just the lower 2 bound 0.159 for a normal distribution. Statistically, it means that $\ln ( t _ { ( 1 , 4 ) } ) { = } { \mu } { - } \sigma$ (where, μ and σ stand for the mean and standard deviation of the normal distribution, respectively). Let the deviation coefficient /=0.05, then $1 \mathrm { n } ( t _ { ( 1 , 4 ) } ) { = } 0 . 9 5 \mu$ and the lower 6 bound can be estimated theoretically as

$$
\ln (t _ {\mu - 3 \sigma}) = \ln (t _ {(1, 4)}) - 2 \sigma = 1 7 \ln (t _ {(1, 4)}) / 1 9\tag{5}
$$

$$
t _ {\mu - 3 \sigma} = e ^ {1 7 \ln (t _ {(1, 4)}) / 1 9}\tag{6}
$$

Obviously, the estimation is sensitive to sample, and the sample outcomes $t _ { ( i , 4 ) } ~ ( i = 1 , 2 , . . . , 4 )$ will suffer considerable change over samples. To modify the lower 6 bound estimator to cover the randomness of the outcome, Monte Carlo simulation is performed to generate samples of size 4 from the pertinent normal distribution.

For the sample (3000<sup>+</sup>,4) comprised of four right-censored life data of which the censoring time is 3000h, providing a deviation ratio 0.05, a log-normal distribution N(8.42, 0.422) can be estimated, and the theoretical value of - is 8.01. However, the sample outcomes shown in Fig.1 illustrate that there is a quite large variation range from about 7.06 to 8.96.

Fig.1 Sample outcomes of the minimum in a sample of size 4 of a normal distribution

According to the simulation result, i.e. the factor that the sample outcome $t _ { ( i , 4 ) }$ will vary between (8.01-0.95) and (8.01+0.95), the confidence level that $t _ { ( i , 4 ) }$ will be inside this range is quite high, depending on how many sample outcomes are used to get the range (800 sample outcomes were drawn and checked in the present paper, shown in Fig.1 are 80 sample outcomes only). Therefore, to conservatively estimate the lower 6 bound, the value of $t _ { ( i , 4 ) }$ in Eq.6 should be reduce an amount being equals to the half range, i.e. a conservative estimator is

$$
t _ {\mu - 3 \sigma} = e ^ {1 7 (\ln (t _ {(1, 4)}) - 0. 9 5) / 1 9}\tag{7}
$$

For engineering application, the lower 6 bound can be taken as the minimal life, i.e. the lower limit of the life random variable. Therefore, a Weibull location parameter estimator turns out. For the situation of the sample (t<sup>+</sup>,4), the estimator is

$$
\gamma = e ^ {1 7 (\ln (t ^ {+}) - 0. 9 5) / 1 9}\tag{8}
$$

## III. BEARING LIFE DISTRIBUTION AND RELIABILITY ESTIMATION AND RESULT DISCUSSION

As mentioned above, the information available for the ball bearing life distribution and reliability estimation includes the shape parameter =2.0 and the single right-censored life time at t=3000h for all the four bearings, no failure occurs.

## A. Parameter estimation and results comparison

The location parameter of the three-parameter Weibull distribution can be estimated by Eq.8 as 552.0h, then the scale parameter is estimated by Eq.4 as 2828.7h.

Firstly, the appropriateness of the bearing life distribution estimated according to the four right-censored observations (3000<sup>+</sup>,4) can be roughly judged, at least conservatively, based on expert experience or historical data. From an engineering viewpoint of view, life dispersion range can be characterized by a lower limit (a very low percentile) and an upper limit (a very high percentile), and the ratio of the upper limit to the lower limit can be used as an index to judge a particular distribution for its appropriateness to a certain product. For instance, a ratio of 10, the quotient of the upper limit corresponding to the probability of 0.99865 to the lower limit corresponding to the probability of 0.00135 can be conservatively settled as the possible maximum for the operation life of the high-quality ball bearings.

That is, although the theoretical scatter range of a Weibull distributed life random variable is infinity, a probability of 99.73%, i.e. the 6 interval is sufficiently large to cover all the possible lives for an industrial product.

For the three-parameter Weibull distribution estimated above, the limit life ratio t<sub>0.99865</sub>/t<sub>0.00135</sub> equals to 11.93, that is big enough for high quality, common used ball bearings, meaning that the three-parameter Weibull distribution is conservative to describe the bearing life random variable in the aspect of dispersion.

In contrast, if the location parameter is assumed zero, i.e. a two-parameter Weibull distribution is applied, the estimated scale parameter equals to 3466.6h. Correspondingly, the limit life ratio t<sub>0.99865</sub>/t<sub>0.00135</sub> equals to 69.94, such a high life dispersion does not seem practical.

Shown in Fig.2 are the three-parameter Weibull life probability density function and the two-parameter Weibull life probability density function estimated according the four rightcensored life data, respectively. Their differences are considerable. Correspondingly, the estimated reliabilities over 1000h operation are 0.975 and 0.920, respectively. The considerable differences highlight the significance of applying three-parameter Weibull probability density function to estimate product reliability.

## B. Estimation result verification by Monte Carlo sampling

To testify the estimation result further, Monte Carlo sampling technique is applied. Shown in Fig.3 are a great amount of Monte Carlo sample outcomes of the minimal outcome $t _ { ( 1 , n ) }$ in samples of size 4 (expressed by the points) drawn from the above estimated Weibull distributions, and the censoring life time (expressed by the straight lines). Even through 3000h was the minimum in a life sample containing 4 observations, most of the sample outcomes (i.e. the points in Fig.3) should also be above it (the straight line in Fig.). By the fact that about 45% and 50% of the sample outcomes are under the minimal observation value for the three-parameter Weibull distribution and the two-parameter Weibull distribution respectively, a judgement can be made that both the threeparameter Weibull distribution based product life estimation and the two-parameter Weibull distribution based product life estimation are conservative, but the latter is more conservative.

## IV. CONCLUSIONS

Three-parameter Weibull distribution should be applied to describe the life of mechanical product. A new method is presented to estimate Weibull location parameter according to very small size sample of right-censored life data, where the censoring time is conservatively taken as the minimal life observation, and the lower 6 bound of the normal distributed log-life is taken as the lower limit of the life random variable from engineering point of view, that is also the Weibull location parameter. The location parameter estimator is built by means of normal distribution based non-parametric estimation method, and the observation value used in the estimator is modified by Monte Carlo simulation result to cover the uncertainty of the observation. Besides, expert experience is utilized to choose a reasonable and conservative product dispersion degree and evaluate the estimation result. As an example, the three-parameter Weibull life distribution of a type of ball bearing is estimated according to four right-censored life data.

Besides, simulated big data generated by Monte Carlo sampling are applied to testify the estimation results, and the appropriateness of the estimated three-parameter Weibull distribution is discussed, all the related knowledge and information available illustrate that the three-parameter Weibull distribution estimated by the new method is better than the two-parameter Weibull distribution conventionally estimated.
