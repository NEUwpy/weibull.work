# Statistical inference about the location parameter of the three-parameter Weibull distribution

Dongming Chen and Zhenmin Chen\*

Department ofStatistics, Florida International University, Miami, FL 33199, USA

(Received 18 September 2006;final version received 23 April 2007)

Exact confidence intervals, confidence limits and point estimators for the location parameter μ of the threeparameter Weibull distributions have been investigated in the literature. One of the purposes of this paper is to find the best selection of i, j and k for the approach given by Chen [Z. Chen, Exact confidence intervals and joint confidence regions for the parameters of the Weibull distributions, Int. J. Reliab., Qual. Safety Eng. 11 (2004), pp. 133–140.] for constructing an exact confidence interval of the location parameter μ. Statistical simulation has been conducted to find the optimal combination. The critical values of the pivotal quantity ω are obtained. The point estimation for the location parameter of the three-parameter Weibull distributions is also discussed. Compared with the commonly used maximum likelihood estimation method, the method introduced in this research provides a simpler, more accurate and more efficient way to estimate the location parameter of the three-parameter Weibull distributions

Keywords: three-parameter Weibull distribution; confidence interval; upper confidence limit; poin estimation; Monte Carlo simulation

## 1. Introduction

The three-parameter Weibull distribution is one of the most widely used lifetime distributions in reliability engineering. This distribution is commonly used as the model of failure-time in life tests and of breakdown-voltage in voltage endurance experiments. An important objective of breakdown-voltage experiments is to estimate either the threshold below which no breakdown occurs or other voltage levels below which breakdowns occur with a small probability. The probability density function of the three-parameter Weibull distribution is

$$
f (x) \frac {\beta}{\eta} \left(\frac {x - \mu}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta - 1} \mathrm{e} ^ {- ((x - \mu) / \eta) ^ {\beta}} \quad x > \mu ,\tag{1}
$$

where $\mu \geq 0$ is the location parameter, $\beta > 0$ the shape parameter and $\eta > 0$ the scale parameter. The Weibull distribution is a versatile distribution that can take on the characteristics of some other types of distributions. It can be used to model a variety of life behaviours based on the values of the parameters. When $\mu = 0$ , the distribution reduces to a two-parameter Weibull distribution. When $\beta = 1$ , it is the case of a two-parameter exponential distribution. Many authors have contributed to the parameter estimation problems for these distributions; see, for examples, Mann [1], Chen [2] and Yang et al. [3].

The difference between the three-parameter Weibull distribution and the two-parameter Weibull distribution is the location parameter $\mu .$ , which shifts the distribution along the x-axis. The inclusion of the location parameter, however, causes some technical difficulties and makes valid inferences on the parameters or functions of the parameters particularly difficult (see [4]). Maximum likelihood estimates (MLEs) of these parameters based on continuous data and their large-sample statistical properties have been extensively studied in the literature. MLEs, however, are difficult to compute numerically, and it is even much harder to construct valid confidence intervals around these estimates. And those difficulties in applying the maximum likelihood method to the three-parameter Weibull distribution models due to non-regularity have led to a variety of alternative approaches in the literature. Many authors, such as Lockhart and Stephens [5] and Schmid [6], have proposed approaches that are alternatives to the MLEs.

Recently, several different approaches have been proposed for constructing confidence intervals and joint confidence regions for the parameters of the Weibull distributions. Some methods are for exact confidence intervals and exact joint confidence regions, whereas the others are for approximate confidence intervals and approximate joint confidence regions. Chen [7] proposed a method for constructing exact confidence intervals andjoint confidence regions for the parameters of the two-parameter exponential distributions and the two-parameter Weibull distributions. In this research, exact confidence intervals, confidence limits and point estimation for the location parameter of the three-parameter Weibull distribution are discussed.

## 2. Statistical inference about μ

The cumulative distribution function of the three-parameter Weibull distribution is given by

$$
F (x) = 1 - \mathrm{e} ^ {((x - \mu) / \eta) ^ {\beta}} \quad x > \mu ,\tag{2}
$$

where $\mu \geq 0$ is the location parameter, $\beta > 0$ the shape parameter and $\eta > 0$ the scale parameter. Here, it is assumed that all the three parameters are unknown. Let $X _ { 1 } , \ldots , X _ { n }$ be a random sample from population distribution (2). Also let $X _ { ( 1 ) } , \ldots , X _ { ( n ) }$ be the corresponding order statistics. It can be seen that

$$
\left(\frac {X _ {i} - \mu}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta}
$$

has a standard exponential distribution $( i = 1 , 2 , \dots , n )$ . Thus,

$$
\left(\frac {X _ {(1)} - \mu}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta}, \left(\frac {X _ {(2)} - \mu}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta}, \dots , \left(\frac {X _ {(n)} - \mu}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta}
$$

are the order statistics of a random sample from the standard exponential distribution. An approach for constructing an exact confidence interval for the location parameter was proposed by Chen [7]. For $1 \leq i < j < k \leq n$ , define

$$
\omega (\mu) = \frac {\ln (X _ {(j)} - \mu) - \ln (X _ {(i)} - \mu)}{\ln (X _ {(k)} - \mu) - \ln (X _ {(j)} - \mu)}.\tag{3}
$$

The expression of $\omega ( \mu )$ can be rewritten as

$$
\omega (\mu) = \frac {\ln ((X _ {(j)} - \mu) / \eta) ^ {\beta} - \ln ((X _ {(i)} - \mu) / \eta) ^ {\beta}}{\ln ((X _ {(k)} - \mu) / \eta) ^ {\beta} - \ln ((X _ {(j)} - \mu) / \eta) ^ {\beta}}.\tag{4}
$$

It can be observed that the distribution of $\omega ( \mu )$ does not depend on parameters $\beta , \eta$ and $\mu$ . It can also be shown that $\omega ( \mu )$ is strictly increasing in $\mu$ . Then the quantiles of $\omega ( \mu )$ can be obtained by the Monte Carlo simulation. Let $\omega _ { \alpha }$ be the α upper quantile of $\omega ( \mu )$ . The values of $\omega _ { \alpha }$ computed based on 250,000 pseudo-random samples of size 40 from the standard three-parameter Weibull distribution are listed in Table 1. Here, the standard three-parameter Weibull distribution refers to the three-parameter Weibull distribution with parameters $\mu = 0 , \eta = 1$ and $\beta = 1 . \operatorname { A }$ confidence interval of $\mu$ can then be constructed based on $\omega ( \mu )$ . In fact,

$$
P (\omega_ {1 - \alpha / 2} <   \omega (\mu) <   \omega_ {\alpha / 2}) = 1 - \alpha\tag{5}
$$

for any $0 < \alpha < 1$ . Then, a $1 - \alpha$ confidence interval for the location parameter $\mu$ can be expressed as $( \mu _ { \mathrm { L } } , \mu _ { \mathrm { U } } )$ , where $\mu _ { \mathrm { L } }$ is the unique solution of $\mu$ for the equation

$$
\omega (\mu) = \omega_ {1 - \alpha / 2},\tag{6}
$$

and $\mu _ { \mathrm { U } }$ is the unique solution of $\mu$ for the equation

$$
\omega (\mu) = \omega_ {\alpha / 2}.\tag{7}
$$

The method can also be used to test hypotheses about the location parameter $\mu .$ . The critical values of $\omega ( \mu )$ for different sample sizes can also be obtained by the Monte Carlo simulation similarly.

Confidence intervals can provide useful information about the parameters of interest to statistics researchers and practitioners. It should be mentioned here that, in some situations, one might have to use one-sided confidence limits instead oftwo-sided confidence intervals. To show this, consider the definition of $\omega ( \mu )$ . Note that $\omega ( \mu )$ is strictly increasing in $\mu$ as mentioned above. Numerically, ω $( \mu )$ obtains its minimum value when $\mu$ approaches its smallest possible value 0. In fact,

$$
\lim _ {\mu \to 0 +} \omega (\mu) = \lim _ {\mu \to 0 +} \frac {\ln (x _ {(j)} - \mu) - \ln (x _ {(i)} - \mu)}{\ln (x _ {(k)} - \mu) - \ln (x _ {(j)} - \mu)} = \frac {\ln (x _ {(j)}) - \ln (x _ {(i)})}{\ln (x _ {(k)}) - \ln (x _ {(j)})}.\tag{8}
$$

Hence,

$$
\omega_ {c} = \frac {\ln (x _ {(j)}) - \ln (x _ {(i)})}{\ln (x _ {(k)}) - \ln (x _ {(j)})}\tag{9}
$$

is the minimum value that $\omega ( \mu )$ can reach, and thus is a very important number. For the specified $x _ { ( i ) } , x _ { ( j ) }$ and $x _ { ( k ) }$

$$
\omega (\mu) > \frac {\ln (x _ {(j)}) - \ln (x _ {(i)})}{\ln (x _ {(k)}) - \ln (x _ {(j)})}\tag{10}
$$

is true for any $0 \leq \mu < x _ { ( i ) }$ . If

$$
\omega_ {1 - \alpha / 2} <   \frac {\ln (x _ {(j)}) - \ln (x _ {(i)})}{\ln (x _ {(k)}) - \ln (x _ {(j)})}\tag{11}
$$

or

$$
\omega_ {\alpha / 2} <   \frac {\ln (x _ {(j)}) - \ln (x _ {(i)})}{\ln (x _ {(k)}) - \ln (x _ {(j)})},\tag{12}
$$

then Equations (6) and $( 7 )$ cannot be solved. In other words, two-sided confidence intervals cannot be constructed in those cases. Simulation result shows that the case of

$$
\omega_ {\alpha / 2} <   \frac {\ln (x _ {(j)}) - \ln (x _ {(i)})}{\ln (x _ {(k)}) - \ln (x _ {(j)})}
$$

is very rare for any reasonable values of α when i, j and $k$ are appropriately selected (see below). However, the proportion of the simulated samples satisfying

$$
\omega_ {1 - \alpha / 2} <   \frac {\ln (x _ {(j)}) - \ln (x _ {(i)})}{\ln (x _ {(k)}) - \ln (x _ {(j)})}
$$

is not a small number, and thus cannot be ignored. Therefore, one-sided upper confidence limit of $\mu$ is recommended. The two-sided confidence intervals is used to find point estimates of $\mu .$ . It is shown by simulation that the one-sided upper confidence limits of $\mu$ and the point estimates of $\mu$ obtained using the methods of this research are surprisingly accurate.

As mentioned above, the selection of $i , j$ and k has significant effect on the performance of the confidence limits. An optimal selection of $i , j$ and k is then desirable to improve the performance of the confidence limits for given confidence level $1 - \alpha$ . It is natural to expect that the smallest order statistic would play an important role in estimating the location parameter $\mu .$ . In other words, one should choose i to be 1. This can actually be verified by the simulation results. After analysing the results of extensive simulation, it has been found that the optimal selection of $i , j$ and k is as follows:

$$
i = 1, \quad k = n \quad \text { and } \quad j = \left[ \frac {(n + 2)}{3} \right].
$$

Here, $[ ( n + 2 ) / 3 ]$ is the integer part of $( n + 2 ) / 3$

In Table 3, the averages of simulated 95% upper confidence limits of $\mu$ are listed for different sample sizes n 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 and 100. The simulation result confirms that $i = 1 , k = n$ and $j = [ ( n + 2 ) / 3 ]$ are always the optimal selection of $i , j$ and k with the proportion of

$$

$$

being almost 0. The optimal selections of $i , j$ and k are highlighted in the tables.

For type II censored data, the above method is still appropriate. In this case, only the first r small est order statistics are observed. Statistical simulation shows that $i = 1 , k = r \operatorname { a n d } j = [ ( r + 2 ) / 3 ]$ are still the optimal selection of i, j and k. ## 3. Point estimation of $\pmb { \mu }$

Point estimation of the parameters of the Weibull distributions is usually conducted by using the MLE methods. MLEs of parameters based on continuous data from the Weibull distribution and large-sample statistical properties have been extensively studied in the literature. However, MLEs are not easy to compute numerically. Furthermore, study shows that even for moderately sized samples from the Weibull model with positive parameters, there is substantial probability that the constrained MLE falls on the boundary $\mu = 0$ , whereas the unconstrained MLE of $\mu$ diverges to . As mentioned above, MLE is not only hard to compute numerically, but also not unique and does not exist under some situations. Because $\mu \leq x$ is true for all $x ,$ , the commonly used alterative method, which takes the estimate as the minimum ordered statistic $x _ { ( 1 ) }$ , is inaccurate. To circumvent such difficulties with the MLE approach, Cheng and Iles [8] proposed reparameterizing the three-parameter Weibull model as a generalized extreme-value (GEV) family, so that infinite values of parameter estimates in the original three-parameter Weibull model reduce to finite values of the parameters of a Gumbel Model in the GEV family. Thus, the process for finding estimators is still very complicated.

In this research, the point estimator for the location parameter of the three-parameter Weibull distributions can easily be obtained by setting the confidence level to $1 - \alpha = 0$ when the method discussed in section $^ 2$ is used. When the confidence level $1 - \alpha = 0$ is set, the lower and upper confidence limits will merge to a point, i.e. the estimated confidence interval becomes the point estimate. Unlike the MLE, obviously the point estimate described here does exist and is unique.

## 4. Conclusion and discussion

In this research, exact confidence intervals, confidence limits and point estimators for the location parameter $\mu$ of the three-parameter Weibull distributions have been investigated. The approach proposed by Chen [7] for constructing an exact confidence interval for the location parameter has been employed and fully discussed.

The critical values of $\omega ( \mu )$ for different sample sizes are provided based on Monte Carlo simulation for some selected combinations of $i , j$ and k. The critical values do not depend on the parameters, but they are complicated functions of $\alpha , n , i , j$ and k.

Exact confidence intervals and confidence limits for the location parameter $\mu$ have been investigated. Compared with other estimation methods, one of the advantages of the estimation method described in this research is its ability to estimate the location of the three-parameter Weibull distribution even for censored samples. Since, in some cases, there is a large probability that the lower confidence bound of the confidence interval cannot be found using this method, the confidence limits become a reasonable choice. Statistical simulation was carried out for different selections of i, j and k with specified confidence level, sample size and parameters.

The results show that the method adopted in this research works well in estimating the location parameter.

The optimization of the combination of $i , j$ and k has been discussed. Extensive simulation results show that when $i = 1 , k = n$ and $j = [ ( n + 2 ) / 3 ]$ , the upper confidence limits of $\mu$ are always the closest to the real value of $\mu$ . Similar results are obtained for selecting optimal combination of $i , j$ and k when censored data are given.

As expected, the sample size has some influence on the accuracy of the estimation of the parameters. The estimation method described in this research shows its accuracy even for small sample sizes. With the increase of the sample size $n ,$ , the upper confidence limit decreases, i.e., the upper confidence limit is closer to its actual value of the parameter.

The point estimator for the location parameter of the three-parameter Weibull distributions is explored in this paper. The results show high accuracy of the point estimator. Compared with the commonly used MLE method, the method introduced in this research provides a simpler, more accurate and more efficient way to estimate the location parameter of the three-parameter Weibull distributions.

In this research, the selection of i, j and k is important for the performance of the described estimation. Further study should be done to theoretically verify that the method of optimal selection adopted in this research is appropriate and accurate. This research focuses on the statistical inference about the location parameter of the three-parameter distribution. It would be useful if a joint confidence region of all three parameters can be constructed.
