# EXACT CONFIDENCE INTERVALS AND JOINT CONFIDENCE REGIONS FOR THE PARAMETERS OF THE WEIBULL DISTRIBUTIONS

ZHENMIN CHEN

Department of Statistics,

Florida International University,

Miami, FL 33199, USA

chenzh@fiu.edu

Received 15 August 2003

Revised 30 January 2004

The Weibull distribution is widely adopted as a lifetime distribution. One of the char acteristics the Weibull distribution possesses is that its cumulative distribution function can be expressed by closed form. Parameter estimation for the Weibull distribution has been discussed by many authors. Various methods have been proposed for constructing confidence intervals and joint confidence regions for the parameters of the Weibull dis tribution based on censored data. This paper discusses those methods that deal with exact confidence intervals or exact joint confidence regions for the parameters. One o the applications of the joint confidence regions of the parameters is to find confidence bounds for the functions of the parameters. In this paper, confidence bounds for the mean lifetime and reliability function for the Weibull distributions are discussed. Some unresolved problems for the exact confidences and joint confidence regions are mentioned in the discussion section

Keywords: Weibull distribution; confidence interval; joint confidence region; reliability function; mean lifetime; unresolved problems.

## 1. Introduction

The probability density function of the three-parameter Weibull distribution is:

$$
f (x) = \frac {\beta}{\eta} \left(\frac {x - \mu}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta - 1} e ^ {- (\frac {x - \mu}{\eta}) ^ {\beta}} \quad (x > \mu)\tag{1}
$$

where $\beta > 0$ is the shape parameter, $\eta > 0$ is the scale parameter, and $- \infty < \mu <$ ∞ is the location parameter. When $\mu = 0$ , the distribution becomes a two-parameter Weibull distribution. When $\beta = 1$ , it is the case of a two-parameter exponential distribution. Many authors have contributed to the parameter estimation problems for these distributions. See, for examples, Mann,<sup>6</sup> Thoman, Bain and Antle,<sup>7</sup> Engelhardt,<sup>4</sup> Lawless,<sup>5</sup> Chao and Hwang,<sup>1</sup> Xu, Fei and Chen,<sup>8</sup> Chen,<sup>2</sup> Chen,<sup>3</sup> and

Yang, See and $\mathrm { X i e . ^ { 9 } }$ There are several diferent ways to construct confidence intervals and joint confidence regions for the parameters of the Weibull distributions. Some methods are for exact confidence intervals and exact joint confidence regions, while the others are for approximate confidence intervals and joint confidence regions. In this paper, only exact confidence intervals and exact joint confidence regions for the parameters are discussed. All the confidence intervals and joint confidence regions discussed in this paper can be constructed based on type II censored data. For example, the confidence intervals and joint confidence regions can be constructed based on the first k order statistics of a random sample from the Weibul distribution population. One of the applications of the joint confidence regions of the parameters is to find confidence bounds for the functions of the parameters. In this paper, confidence bounds for the mean lifetime and reliability function for the Weibull distributions are discussed. In the discussion section, some unresolved problems for the exact confidences and joint confidence regions are discussed.

## 2. Three-Parameter Weibull Distribution

The cumulative distribution function of the three-parameter Weibull distribution is

$$
F (x) = 1 - e ^ {- (\frac {x - \mu}{\eta}) ^ {\beta}} \quad (x > \mu)\tag{2}
$$

where $- \infty < \mu < \infty$ is the location parameter, $\beta > 0$ is the shape parameter and $\eta > 0$ is the scale parameter. Here it is assumed that all the three parameters are unknown. In this section, exact confidence interval for $\mu$ is discussed. Let $X _ { 1 } , \ldots , X _ { n }$ be a random sample from population distribution (2), and let $X _ { ( 1 ) } , \dots , X _ { ( n ) }$ be the corresponding order statistics. It can be seen that

$$
\left(\frac {x _ {i} - \mu}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta}
$$

has a standard exponential distribution $( i = 1 , 2 , \ldots , n )$ . Thus

$$
\left(\frac {x _ {(1)} - \mu}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta}, \left(\frac {x _ {(2)} - \mu}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta}, \ldots , \left(\frac {x _ {(n)} - \mu}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta}
$$

are the order statistics of a random sample from the standard exponential distri bution. For $1 \leq i < j < k \leq n$ , define

$$
\omega (\mu) = \frac {\ln (X _ {(j)} - \mu) - \ln (X _ {(i)} - \mu)}{\ln (X _ {(k)} - \mu) - \ln (X _ {(j)} - \mu)}.\tag{3}
$$

The expression of $\omega ( \mu )$ can be rewritten as:

$$
\omega (\mu) = \frac {\ln \left[ \frac {\left(\frac {X _ {(j)} - \mu}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta}}{\left(\frac {X _ {(i)} - \mu}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta}} \right]}{\ln \left[ \frac {\left(\frac {X _ {(k)} - \mu}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta}}{\left(\frac {X _ {(j)} - \mu}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta}} \right]}.\tag{4}
$$

It is easy to see that the distribution of $\omega ( \mu )$ does not depend on any parameters. It can also be shown that $\omega ( \mu )$ is strictly increasing in $\mu .$ . Then a confidence interval can be constructed based on $\omega ( \mu )$ . In fact, let $\omega _ { \alpha }$ be the upper $\alpha$ critical value of the distribution of $\omega ( \mu )$ . Then

$$
P (\omega_ {1 - \alpha / 2} <   \omega (\mu) <   \omega_ {\alpha / 2}) = 1 - \alpha\tag{5}
$$

for any $0 < \alpha < 1$ . Thus a $1 - \alpha$ confidence interval for the location parameter $\mu$ is $( \mu _ { L } , \mu _ { U } )$ where $\mu _ { L }$ is the unique solution of $\mu$ for the equation

$$
\omega (\mu) = \omega_ {1 - \alpha / 2}
$$

and $\mu _ { U }$ is the unique solution of $\mu$ for the equation

$$
\omega (\mu) = \omega_ {\alpha / 2}.
$$

Statement (5) can be used to test hypotheses about the location parameter $\mu .$ For example, the test $H _ { 0 } \colon \mu = 0$ versus $H _ { a } \colon \mu \neq 0$ can be used to recognize twoparameter Weibull distribution from the family of general three-parameter Weibull distributions. Based on the above discussion, $H _ { 0 }$ is rejected if $\omega ( 0 ) < \omega _ { 1 - \alpha / 2 }$ or $\omega ( 0 ) > \omega _ { \alpha / 2 }$ . Critical values of $\omega ( \mu )$ can be obtained by the Monte-Carlo simulation.

## 3. Two-Parameter Exponential Distribution

The two-parameter exponential distribution can be treated as a special case of the three-parameter Weibull distribution by letting $\beta = 1$ . The probability density function of the two-parameter exponential distribution is

$$
f (x) = \frac {1}{\eta} e ^ {- \frac {x - \mu}{\eta}} \quad (x > \mu).\tag{6}
$$

A joint confidence region for the parameters $\mu$ and $\eta$ can be constructed based on the first k order statistics of a random sample from the two-parameter exponential distribution using the following results:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \text {(1)} \frac {2 [ \sum_ {i = 2} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} + (1 - n) X _ {(1)} ]}{\eta} \sim \chi_ {2 k - 2} ^ {2} \\ \text {(2)} \frac {2 n (X _ {(1)} - \mu)}{\eta} \sim \chi_ {2} ^ {2} \\ \text {(3)} \frac {2 [ \sum_ {i = 2} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} + (1 - n) X _ {(1)} ]}{\eta} \text {and} \frac {2 n (X _ {(1)} - \mu)}{\eta} \text {are independent.} \end{array}
$$

Then the following statements hold:

$$
\begin{array}{l l} (1) & \frac {2 [ \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} - n \mu ]}{\eta} \sim \chi_ {2 k} ^ {2} \\ (2) & \frac {\sum_ {i = 2} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} + (1 - n) X _ {(1)}}{n (k - 1) (X _ {(1)} - \mu)} \sim F _ {2 k - 2, 2} \\ (3) & \frac {2 [ \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} - n \mu ]}{\eta} \quad \text { and } \quad \frac {\sum_ {i = 2} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} + (1 - n) X _ {(1)}}{n (k - 1) (X _ {(1)} - \mu)} \\ & \text { independent. } \end{array}\tag{are}
$$

A $1 - \alpha$ joint confidence region can then be constructed as

$$
\left\{ \begin{array}{l} X _ {(1)} - \frac {\sum_ {i = 2} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} + (1 - n) X _ {(1)}}{n (k - 1) F _ {\frac {1 + \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} (2 k - 2 , 2)} \\ \leq \mu \leq X _ {(1)} - \frac {\sum_ {i = 2} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} + (1 - n) X _ {(1)}}{n (k - 1) F _ {\frac {1 - \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} (2 k - 2 , 2)} \\ \frac {2 [ \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} - n \mu ]}{\chi_ {\frac {1 - \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} ^ {2} (2 k)} \\ \leq \eta \leq \frac {2 [ \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} - n \mu ]}{\chi_ {\frac {1 - \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} ^ {2} (2 k)} \end{array} \right.\tag{7}
$$

The joint confidence region of $\mu$ and $\eta$ can be used to find confidence bounds for the functions of $\mu$ and $\eta .$ . For example, a $1 - \alpha$ lower confidence bound of the reliability function (survival function)

$$
R (t) = e ^ {- \frac {t - \mu}{\eta}}
$$

can be expressed as

$$
\begin{array}{l} \inf \left\{e ^ {- \frac {t - \mu}{\eta}}: X _ {(1)} - \frac {\sum_ {i = 2} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} + (1 - n) X _ {(1)}}{n (k - 1) F _ {\frac {1 + \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} (2 k - 2 , 2)} \right. \\ \qquad \leq \mu \leq X _ {(1)} - \frac {\sum_ {i = 2} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} + (1 - n) X _ {(1)}}{n (k - 1) F _ {\frac {1 - \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} (2 k - 2 , 2)}, \\ \text {and} \eta = \frac {2 [ \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} - n \mu ]}{\chi_ {\sqrt {1 - \alpha}} ^ {2} (2 k)} \Bigg \}. \end{array}\tag{8}
$$

This is because

$$
\left\{ \begin{array}{l} X _ {(1)} - \frac {\sum_ {i = 2} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} + (1 - n) X _ {(1)}}{n (k - 1) F _ {\frac {1 + \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} (2 k - 2 , 2)} \\ \qquad \leq \mu \leq X _ {(1)} - \frac {\sum_ {i = 2} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} + (1 - n) X _ {(1)}}{n (k - 1) F _ {\frac {1 - \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} (2 k - 2 , 2)}, \\ \eta \geq \frac {2 [ \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} - n \mu ]}{\chi_ {\sqrt {1 - \alpha}} ^ {2} (2 k)} \end{array} \right.
$$

is also a $1 - \alpha$ joint confidence region for $\mu$ and $\eta ,$ , and is because $R ( t )$ is increasing in both $\mu$ and $\eta .$ . Note that the region where the infimum is taken is a segment.

## 4. Two-Parameter Weibull Distribution

The cumulative distribution function of a two-parameter Weibull distribution is

$$
F (x) = 1 - e ^ {- (\frac {x}{\eta}) ^ {\beta}} \quad (x > 0)\tag{9}
$$

where $\beta > 0$ is the shape parameter and $\eta > 0$ is the scale parameter. Thoman, Bain and $\mathrm { A n t l e } ^ { 7 }$ developed a method for constructing confidence intervals for $\beta$ and $\eta$ based on the maximum likelihood estimators. Let $\hat { \beta }$ and $\hat { \eta }$ be the maximum likelihood estimators of $\beta$ and $\eta ,$ respectively. It was shown that the distributions of

$$
\frac {\hat {\beta}}{\beta} \text { and } \hat {\beta} \ln \left(\frac {\hat {\eta}}{\eta}\right)
$$

are parameter free. Lower and upper percentiles of ${ \hat { \beta } } / \beta$ and $\hat { \beta } \ln ( \hat { \eta } / \eta )$ are provided in Thoman, Bain and $\mathrm { A n t l e } ^ { 7 }$ using Monte Carlo simulation. Confidence intervals for $\beta$ and $\eta$ can then be constructed based on this result.

Chen<sup>2</sup> proposed a simple method to construct confidence interval and to conduct statistical test for the shape parameter of the two-parameter Weibull distribution. Let $X _ { 1 } , \ldots , X _ { n }$ be a random sample from a two-parameter Weibull distribution. Define

$$
U _ {i} = \left(\frac {X _ {i}}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta} \quad i = 1, \dots , n.
$$

Then $U _ { 1 } , \ldots , U _ { n }$ form a random sample from standard exponential distribution. Let

$$
U _ {(i)} = \left(\frac {X _ {(i)}}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta} \quad i = 1, \dots , n.
$$

It can be seen that $U _ { ( 1 ) } , \ldots , U _ { ( n ) }$ are the corresponding order statistics of $U _ { 1 } , \ldots , U _ { n }$ . Define

$$
U _ {A M} = \frac {\sum_ {i = 1} ^ {k - 1} U _ {(i)} + (n - k + 1) U _ {(k)}}{n}
$$

and

$$
U _ {G M} = \left(\prod_ {i = 1} ^ {k - 1} U _ {(i)} \cdot U _ {(k)} ^ {n - k + 1}\right) ^ {\frac {1}{n}}.
$$

Furthermore, let

$$
\zeta (\beta) = \frac {U _ {A M}}{U _ {G M}} = \frac {\frac {1}{n} (\sum_ {i = 1} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} ^ {\beta} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} ^ {\beta})}{(\prod_ {j = 1} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(j)} \cdot X _ {(k)} ^ {n - k + 1}) ^ {\frac {\beta}{n}}}.\tag{10}
$$

The distribution of $\zeta ( \beta )$ doesn’t depend on any parameter. Let $\zeta _ { \alpha }$ be the upper α critical value of the distribution of $\zeta ( \beta )$ . Then

$$
P (\zeta_ {1 - \alpha / 2} <   \zeta (\beta) <   \zeta_ {\alpha / 2}) = 1 - \alpha
$$

for any $0 < \alpha < 1$ . Note that

$$
\zeta (\beta) = \frac {\frac {1}{n} (\sum_ {i = 1} ^ {k - 1} a _ {i} ^ {\beta} + (n - k + 1) a _ {k} ^ {\beta})}{(\sum_ {j = 1} ^ {k - 1} a _ {j} \cdot a _ {k} ^ {n - k + 1}) ^ {\frac {\beta}{n}}}
$$

is strictly increasing in $\beta > 0$ . Then a $1 - \alpha$ confidence interval for the shape parameter $\beta$ is $( \beta _ { L } , \beta _ { U } )$ where $\beta _ { L }$ is the solution of $\beta$ for the equation

$$
\zeta (\beta) = \zeta_ {1 - \alpha / 2}
$$

and $\beta _ { U }$ is the solution of $\beta$ for the equation

$$
\zeta (\beta) = \zeta_ {\alpha / 2}.
$$

The test $H _ { 0 } \colon \beta = 1$ versus $H _ { a } \colon \beta \neq 1$ is needed to recognize the exponential distri bution from the family of two-parameter Weibull distributions. Based on the above discussion, it can be seen that the decision rule is to reject $H _ { 0 }$ if $\zeta ( 1 ) > \zeta _ { \alpha / 2 }$ or $\zeta ( 1 ) < \zeta _ { 1 - \alpha / 2 }$

An approach for constructing exact joint confidence regions for the parameters of a two-parameter Weibull distribution was proposed in Chen.<sup>3</sup> Define

$$
\zeta (X _ {(1)}, \ldots , X _ {(k)}, \beta) = \frac {\sum_ {i = 2} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} ^ {\beta} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} ^ {\beta} + (1 - n) X _ {(1)} ^ {\beta}}{n (k - 1) X _ {(1)} ^ {\beta}}
$$

and

$$
\delta (X _ {(1)}, \dots , X _ {(k)}, \beta) = \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {k - 1} X _ {(i)} ^ {\beta} + (n - k + 1) X _ {(k)} ^ {\beta}.
$$

Then for any $0 < \alpha < 1$ , the following inequalities determine a $1 - \alpha$ joint confidence region for $\beta$ and $\eta \colon$

$$
\left\{ \begin{array}{l} \varphi (X _ {(1)}, \ldots , X _ {(k)}, F _ {\frac {1 + \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} (2 k - 2, 2)) \leq \beta \leq \varphi (X _ {(1)}, \ldots , X _ {(k)}, F _ {\frac {1 - \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} (2 k - 2, 2)) \\ \left[ \frac {2 \delta (X _ {(1)} , \ldots , X _ {(k)} , \beta)}{\chi_ {\frac {1 - \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} ^ {2} (2 k)} \right] ^ {\frac {1}{\beta}} \leq \eta \leq \left[ \frac {2 \delta (X _ {(1)} , \ldots , X _ {(k)} , \beta)}{\chi_ {\frac {1 + \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} ^ {2} (2 k)} \right] ^ {\frac {1}{\beta}} \end{array} \right.\tag{11}
$$

where $\varphi ( X _ { ( 1 ) } , \dots , X _ { ( k ) } , t )$ is the solution of $\beta$ for the equation

$$
\zeta (X _ {(1)}, \dots , X _ {(k)}, \beta) = t.\tag{12}
$$

The joint confidence region for $\beta$ and $\eta$ can be used to find confidence bounds for any function of $\beta$ and $\eta .$ . For example, suppose that $X _ { ( 1 ) } , \ldots , X _ { ( k ) }$ are the first k order statistics of a sample of size n from a two-parameter Weibull distribution with shape parameter $\beta$ and scale parameter η. Then for any $0 < \alpha < 1$ ,

$$
\inf _ {\beta} \exp \left\{- \frac {t ^ {\beta} \chi_ {\sqrt {1 - \alpha}} ^ {2} (2 k)}{2 \delta (X _ {(1)} , \ldots , X _ {(k)} , \beta)} \right\}\tag{13}
$$

is a $1 - \alpha$ lower confidence bound for the reliability function

$$
R (t) = e ^ {- (\frac {t}{\eta}) ^ {\beta}}
$$

where the infimum is taken over the region

$$
\varphi (X _ {(1)}, \ldots , X _ {(k)}, F _ {\frac {1 + \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} (2 k - 2, 2)) \leq \beta \leq \varphi (X _ {(1)}, \ldots , X _ {(k)}, F _ {\frac {1 - \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} (2 k - 2, 2))\tag{14}
$$

In other words,

$$
P \left(R (t) > \inf _ {\beta} \exp \left\{- \frac {t ^ {\beta} \chi_ {\sqrt {1 - \alpha}} ^ {2} (2 k)}{2 \delta (X _ {(1)} , \ldots , X _ {(k)} , \beta)} \right\}\right) \geq 1 - \alpha .
$$

The mean lifetime of components is one of the important characteristics in reliability analysis. If the underlying distribution is a two-parameter Weibull distribution defined in Eq. (9), then the mean lifetime of the distribution is

$$
\mu = \eta \Gamma \left(\frac {1}{\beta} + 1\right).
$$

It can be shown that

$$
\begin{array}{c} \inf _ {\beta} \exp \left\{\left[ \frac {2 \delta (X _ {(1)} , \ldots , X _ {(k)} , \beta)}{\chi_ {\sqrt {1 - \alpha}} ^ {2} (2 k)} \right] ^ {\frac {1}{\beta}} \Gamma \left(\frac {1}{\beta} + 1\right): \right. \\ \varphi (X _ {(1)}, \ldots , X _ {(k)}, F _ {\frac {1 + \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} (2 k - 2, 2)) \\ \leq \beta \leq \varphi (X _ {(1)}, \ldots , X _ {(k)}, F _ {\frac {1 - \sqrt {1 - \alpha}}{2}} (2 k - 2, 2)) \Bigg \} \end{array}\tag{15}
$$

is a $1 - \alpha$ lower confidence bound for the mean lifetime of the two-parameter Weibull distribution.

## 5. Discussion

The Weibull distribution has its long history. It is widely adopted as a lifetime distribution. One of the characteristics the Weibull distributions possess is that the cumulative distributions of the distributions can be expressed using closed form. Numerous papers have been published for estimating parameters for both the twoparameter Weibull distribution and the three-parameter Weibull distribution. As mentioned in Sec. 1, this paper only discusses methods for constructing exact confidence intervals and exact joint confidence regions for the parameters of the Weibull distributions. There are still some problems in this area that may attract statistics researchers to work on. Exact joint confidence regions for the parameters of the two-parameter exponential distribution and two-parameter Weibull distribution are given in Eqs. (7) and (11), respectively. The joint confidence regions are constructed based on type II censored samples. It will be interesting if exact joint confidence regions for the parameters can be constructed based on type I censored samples. The same problem can be raised for the three-parameter Weibull distri bution. In other words, an exact joint confidence region for the parameters of the three-parameter Weibull distribution based on type I censored sample or type II will be useful in statistical inference about the parameters. In Eq. (3), there are many diferent choices for the selection of i, j and k. Further research for the selection of i, j and k is still needed. Conservative lower confidence bounds for reliability function R(t) and mean lifetime of the distributions are given in Eqs. (8), (14) and (15). It means that the probability that R(t) or the mean lifetime is greater than the lower confidence bound is at least 1 − α. It should be interesting to see an exact lower confidence limit for R(t) or the mean lifetime such that the probability that R(t) or the mean lifetime is greater than the lower confidence limit is exactly 1 − α.
