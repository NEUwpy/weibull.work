The most applied principles for parameter estimation are maximum likelihood and least square error. This paper presents a new principle with regard to the parameter estimation of the three-parameter Weibull distribution. By transforming the cumulative distribution function, constructed is a mapping from the value of the random variable and its corresponding cumulative distribution probability to the scale parameter. The scale parameter estimated by such a mapping is the random variable value and the corresponding cumulative distribution probability dependent when the shape parameter and/or location parameter applied in the mapping is subject to error. Given a set of random variable values or a set of sample values, a larger error in the shape/location parameter brings about larger differences between the scale parameter values obtained with the individual random variable values or sample values, respectively. Based on such a causal relationship between the discrepancy and the shape/location parameter value applied in the mapping relation, a new parameter estimation method is proposed. For the Weibull distribution parameter estimation according to a set of sample values, the right shape parameter and location parameter are those minimizing the discrepancy between the scale parameter values obtained with the individual sample values, respectively. Case studies demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms the maximum likelihood method and the Weibull plot-based least squares method.

Keywords: Three-parameter Weibull distribution; parameter estimation; pseudo estimator; parameter estimation principle; parameter estimation criterion.

# 1. Introduction

The Weibull distribution has been widely used to describe product lifetimes because of its mathematical tractability and adaptability. The majority of random variables

involved in engineering problems can be well described with Weibull distribution, except for some special cases. $^{1,2}$  Nevertheless, the mostly applied is the two-parameter Weibull distribution. $^{3-6}$  The three-parameter Weibull distribution has more advantages, especially for describing long life and high reliability product. $^{7-9}$  However, more parameters in a probability distribution function mean more complications for parameter estimation. Unless the sample size is large enough, the error of the conventional parameter estimation methods for three-parameter Weibull distributions is quite large and unstable. $^{10}$

Parameter estimation for the three-parameter Weibull distribution has long been a concern for both methodological research and practical application.[11-13] Lots of efforts have been made to propose or revise parameter estimation methods.[14-21] To overcome the shortcomings of the traditional maximum likelihood method in terms of difficult equation-resolving and non-regularity, a variety of alternative approaches were developed.[22-25]

Nagatsuka<sup>9</sup> proposed an efficient method for the estimation of parameters and quantiles of the three-parameter Weibull distribution by using statistics invariant to the unknown location parameter. To estimate the location parameter of the three-parameter Weibull distribution, Sugiyama<sup>26</sup> proposed a minimum-variance linear unbiased estimator based on the order statistics. Ouedraogo et al. proposed a differential evolution optimization method.<sup>27</sup> Jukic et al. proposed a non-linear weighted errors-in-variables fitting approach.<sup>28</sup> Acitas et al. proposed a particle swarm optimization method with a new adaptive search space obtained by the modified maximum likelihood estimators.<sup>29</sup> Yonar et al. proposed a heuristic method by means of the dragonfly algorithm for obtaining the maximum likelihood estimates of the three-parameter Weibull distribution.<sup>30</sup> In addition, the neural network and the genetic algorithm were used to get the best smoothing parameter of the three-parameter Weibull distribution.<sup>31</sup>

By comparing the performance of 12 methods, Teimouri et al. illustrated that the parameters estimated by the different methods differ from each other considerably even in large-sized sample situations.[32] Maurizio and Penta argued that the technical usefulness of the classical (frequentist) statistical analysis is very limited when the sample size is small.[33] Hence, developing accurate, robust, and efficient parameter estimation methods are still of great significance.

# 2. Minimum Discrepancy Principle for Scale Parameter Estimation

### 2.1. A pseudo estimator for the scale parameter of the Weibull distribution

The probability density function and cumulative distribution function of a three-parameter Weibull distributed random variable  $T$  are

$$
f (t) = \frac {\beta}{\eta} \left(\frac {t - \gamma}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta - 1} \exp \left[ - \left(\frac {t - \gamma}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta} \right], \tag {2.1}
$$

$$
F (t) = 1 - \exp \left[ - \left(\frac {t - \gamma}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta} \right], \tag {2.2}
$$

where  $\beta$ ,  $\eta$  and  $\gamma$  stand for the shape parameter, scale parameter and location parameter, respectively.

By Eq. (2.2), a mapping from  $t$  and  $F(t)$  to  $\eta$  can be constructed:

$$
\eta = (t - \gamma) / (- \ln (1 - F (t))) ^ {1 / \beta}. \tag {2.3}
$$

By Eq. (2.3), the Weibull scale parameter can be estimated with a value of the random variable  $t$  and the corresponding cumulative distribution probability  $F(t)$ , given the location parameter  $\gamma$  and the shape parameter  $\beta$ .

When a sample of size  $n$  is available, let  $t_{1,n}, t_{2,n}, \ldots, t_{n,n}$  denote the sample values arranged in ascending order (i.e.  $t_{1,n} \leq t_{2,n} \leq \dots \leq t_{n,n}$ ), the corresponding cumulative distribution probabilities can be estimated by the median rank estimator:

$$
F \left(t _ {i, n}\right) = \frac {i - 0 . 3}{n + 0 . 4}. \tag {2.4}
$$

Therefore, through Eqs. (2.3) and (2.4), the scale parameter can be estimated with a sample value  $t_{i,n}$ , provided that the location parameter  $\gamma$  and shape parameter  $\beta$  are known. In this sense,  $(T_{i,n} - \gamma) / (-\ln(1 - \frac{i - 0.3}{n + 0.4}))^{1/\beta}$  can be regarded as a pseudo estimator for the scale parameter, i.e., a scale parameter value can be estimated with a sample value  $t_{i,n}(i = 1,2,\ldots,n)$  as

$$
\eta_ {i} = \frac {t _ {i , n} - \gamma}{(- \ln (1 - F (t _ {i , n}))) ^ {1 / \beta}}. \tag {2.5}
$$

## 2.2. Scale parameter estimate variation

Since the sample value  $t_{i,n}$  is not exactly the distribution percentile corresponding to the cumulative distribution probability estimated by the median rank estimator, the scale parameters estimated by Eq. (2.5) with different sample values are different from each other. Besides, the differences between the scale parameter values estimated by Eq. (2.5) also depend on the error of the location/shape parameter applied, as the true parameter values are unknown. It is easy to know that the larger the error of the location/shape parameter, the larger the differences between the estimated scale parameter values  $\eta_i (i = 1, 2, \dots, n)$ .

### 2.2.1. The effect of location parameter error

Let  $\Delta \gamma$  denote the error of the location parameter applied in Eq. (2.5), the variation of the estimated scale parameter resulted from  $\Delta \gamma$  is

$$
\Delta \eta_ {i} (\Delta \gamma) = \eta_ {i} (\gamma + \Delta \gamma , \beta) - \eta_ {i} (\gamma , \beta) = \Delta \gamma / \left(- \ln \left(1 - F \left(t _ {i, n}\right)\right)\right) ^ {1 / \beta}, \tag {2.6}
$$

where  $\eta_{i}(\gamma +\Delta \gamma ,\beta)$  stands for the scale parameter estimated by Eq. (2.5) with sample value  $t_{i,n}$  as well as location parameter value  $\gamma +\Delta \gamma$  and shape parameter

Because the  $F(t_{i,n})$  applied in Eq. (2.6) is a function of the ordinal number  $i$  (ref. Eq. (2.4)), the individual  $\Delta \eta_i(\Delta \gamma)$  ( $i = 1, 2, \ldots, n$ ) are different from each other. The maximal difference between  $\Delta \eta_i(\Delta \gamma)$  ( $i = 1, 2, \ldots, n$ ), which is also the maximal difference between the scale parameter estimates  $\eta_i (i = 1, 2, \ldots, n)$ , estimated with the individual sample values  $t_{i,n} (i = 1, 2, \ldots, n)$  is directly proportional to the location parameter error  $\Delta \gamma$ , i.e.

$$
\begin{array}{l} \Delta \eta_ {1} (\gamma + \Delta \gamma , \beta) - \Delta \eta_ {n} (\gamma + \Delta \gamma , \beta) \\ = \Delta \eta_ {1} (\Delta \gamma) - \Delta \eta_ {n} (\Delta \gamma) \\ = \frac {\Delta \gamma}{\left(- \ln \left(1 - F \left(t _ {1 , n}\right)\right)\right) ^ {\frac {1}{\beta}}} - \frac {\Delta \gamma}{\left(- \ln \left(1 - F \left(t _ {n , n}\right)\right)\right) ^ {\frac {1}{\beta}}}. \tag {2.7} \\ \end{array}
$$

$$
\Delta \eta_ {i} (\Delta \beta) = \frac {t _ {i , n} - \gamma}{(- \ln (1 - F (t _ {i , n}))) ^ {\frac {1}{\beta + \Delta \beta}}} - \frac {t _ {i , n} - \gamma}{(- \ln (1 - F (t _ {i , n}))) ^ {\frac {1}{\beta}}}. \tag {2.8}
$$

These variations come from both the error of the shape parameter and the uncertainty of the sample values (different sample values correspond to different errors in the corresponding cumulative distribution probabilities estimated by the median rank estimator). ## 2.3. Principle and procedure to estimate Weibull distribution parameters

As the larger error in the location/shape parameter results in larger difference between the scale parameter estimates associated with the individual sample values, the right location parameter and shape parameter can be identified by a two-dimensional recursion process, with regards to location parameter and shape parameter, respectively. The standard deviation of the  $n$  scale parameter estimates  $\eta_1, \eta_2, \ldots, \eta_n$  obtained from the  $n$  sample values  $t_{1,n}, t_{2,n}, \ldots, t_{1,n}$ , denoted by  $\sigma_{\eta}$ , is used to characterize the difference. For parameter estimation, an initial shape parameter value  $\beta_0$  (a possible minimum shape parameter value) and a suitable increment  $\Delta \beta$  are set first, and a series of shape parameter values  $\beta_j = \beta_0 + j\Delta \beta (j = 0,1,2,\ldots)$  are considered. To each of the shape parameter value  $\beta_j$ , a series of location parameter values  $\gamma_k = k\Delta \gamma$  ( $\Delta \gamma$  is an increment of location parameter  $\gamma$  used in the recursion process,  $k = 0,1,2,\ldots$ ) are tried. Obviously, a pair of  $(\beta_j, \gamma_k)$

Fig. 2. Squared difference between estimated scale parameters  $\eta_{1}(\gamma, \beta + \Delta \beta)$  and  $\eta_{15}(\gamma, \beta + \Delta \beta)$  with respective to the different shape parameter error  $\Delta \beta$ . far from the true parameter values will yield a large  $\sigma_{\eta}$ . The pair of  $(\beta_j, \gamma_k)$  obtained from minimizing the standard deviation  $\sigma_{\eta}$  are the estimated shape parameter  $\hat{\beta}$  and location parameter  $\hat{\gamma}$ .

When the right shape parameter and location parameter are obtained, a scale parameter can be estimated by means of Eq. (2.5) with a sample value. As mentioned above, since the sample value  $t_{i,n}$  is not exactly the  $(i - 0.3) / (n + 0.4) \times 100$ th percentile of the distribution, the scale parameter values estimated by Eq. (2.5) with the individual sample values will differ from each other. In other words, the different pairs of  $(t_{i,n}, F(t_{i,n}))$  yield different scale parameter estimates even though the right shape parameter and location parameter are used. To reduce the effect of sample uncertainty, the average of such  $n$  estimates  $\eta_i (i = 1, 2, \dots, n)$  is taken as the final estimate of the scale parameter:

$$
\eta = \frac {1}{n} \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {n} \frac {t _ {i , n} - \hat {\gamma}}{(- \ln (1 - F (t _ {i , n}))) ^ {1 / \hat {\beta}}}. \tag {2.10}
$$

# 3. Weibull Distribution Parameter Estimation

### 3.1. Procedures for Weibull distribution parameter estimation

The procedures to estimate the three parameters of the Weibull distribution according to a sample of size 15 are described below. The 15 sample values, 1372.9, 1464.2, 1980.6, 1899.9, 1716.2, 2097.6, 2105.0, 1615.6, 1657.6, 1687.8, 2170.8, 2446.1, 2274.7, 1573.9 and 2639.3, are generated by Monte Carlo sampling from the Weibull distribution  $W$  (2.0, 1000, 1000). To roughly estimate the parameters, five potential shape parameter values, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0, and 4.0, are considered (smaller initial value and smaller increment are necessary for higher accuracy). To each of the shape parameter value, a series of potential location parameter values, 0, 50, 100, ... (smaller increment should be used for higher accuracy), are tried to get a series of standard deviations of the scale parameter estimates  $\eta_i(\beta_j,\gamma_k)(i = 1,2,\dots ,15)$  estimated by means of Eq. (2.5) with the 15 sample values, respectively. For each shape parameter value  $\beta_{j}$  and a series of location parameter values  $\gamma_{k}(k = 1,2,\ldots)$ , a series of  $\sigma_{\eta}(\beta_{j}, \gamma_{k})(k = 1,2,\ldots)$  can be obtained. The results are shown in Fig. 3, where each curve represents a relationship between  $\sigma_{\eta}(\beta_j,\gamma_k)(k = 1,2,\dots ,15)$  and  $\gamma_{k}$  for a particular shape parameter value  $\beta_{j}$ . A conditional minimal standard deviation of the estimated scale parameters  $\sigma_{\eta ,\mathrm{min}}(\beta_j)$  can be obtained for each of the  $\beta_{j}$ . The minimal standard deviation of the estimated scale parameters, i.e.  $\mathrm{Min}\{\sigma_{\eta ,\mathrm{min}}(\beta_j), j = 1,2,\dots ,J\}$  ( $J$  is the total number of the shape parameter values considered), denoted by  $\sigma_{\eta ,\mathrm{min}}$ , is achieved at  $\beta = 2.0$ ,  $\gamma = 1200$ . Therefore, the shape parameter is estimated as 2.0 and the location parameter is estimated as 1200. Based on the estimated shape parameter and location parameter, the scale parameter is estimated by Eq. (2.10) as 806.0.

Comparing with the true values of the Weibull distribution parameters, i.e.  $\beta = 2.0$ ,  $\eta = 1000$  and  $\gamma = 1000$ , the relative errors are  $0\%$ ,  $20\%$  and  $-19.4\%$  for the estimated shape parameter, location parameter and scale parameter, respectively.

### 3.2. Minimum discrepancy criterion

As described above,  $n$  scale parameter values can be obtained, respectively, with  $n$  sample values by Eq. (2.5) with given shape parameter and location parameter. Besides the fact that the uncertainty of the sample values makes the scale parameter estimates differ from each other, the errors of the shape parameter and location parameter used in Eq. (2.5) bring about additional discrepancy between such estimated scale parameter values. For a given parameter value  $\beta$ , a minimal standard deviation of the estimated scale parameter values (denoted by  $\sigma_{\eta,\min}(\beta)$ ) exists at a certain location parameter value. Shown in Fig. 4 is a typical case for a sample of size 15. Figure 4(a) is the relationship between  $\sigma_{\eta,\min}(\beta)$  and the shape parameter  $\beta$ . Figure 4(b) is the decrease rate (the slope) of  $\sigma_{\eta,\min}(\beta)$  with respect to the shape parameter  $\beta$ . The decrease rate is numerically defined as

$$
\frac {\partial \sigma_ {\operatorname* {m i n}} (\beta)}{\partial \beta} = \frac {\sigma_ {\operatorname* {m i n}} (\beta) - \sigma_ {\operatorname* {m i n}} (\beta + \Delta \beta)}{\Delta \beta}. \tag {3.1}
$$

As  $\sigma_{\eta,\min}(\beta)$  gets its minimum at  $\partial \sigma_{\eta,\min}(\beta) / \partial \beta = 0$ , the "minimum discrepancy criterion" for scale parameter estimation is expressed as

$$
\frac {\partial \sigma_ {\min } (\beta)}{\partial \beta} = 0. \tag {3.2}
$$

Based on this criterion, the Weibull distribution parameters  $\beta$  and  $\gamma$  minimizing the standard deviation of the estimated scale parameter values  $\sigma_{\eta}(\beta, \gamma)$  can be found by a recursion process (e.g. using a shape parameter increment  $\Delta \beta = 0.1$ , starting at an initial value 0.1, and a location parameter increment  $\Delta \gamma = 50$ , starting at 0). The dotted line in Fig. 4(c) is the fitted relationship between  $\partial \sigma_{\eta, \min}(\beta) / \partial \beta$  and  $\beta$ , where the built-in cubic polynomial function in EXCEL, Microsoft Office, is used. ### 3.3. Parameter estimation results according to samples of size 15

To compare the MD method with the conventional methods (maximum likelihood method and Weibull plot correlation coefficient-based least squares method, denoted by ML method and LS method, respectively), 45 samples are used. Shown in Fig. 5 are the parameter estimation results of 30 samples of size 15, generated by Monte Carol sampling from  $W$  (2.0, 1000, 1000), where MDE, MLE and LSE stand for the estimates of the MD method, ML method and LS method, respectively. Among the 30 samples, there are six cases where ML method fails to get solution. These cases are marked with zero parameter values in Fig. 5.

The parameter estimation results show that, in more than two-thirds of the samples, the shape parameters estimated by MD method are closer to the true value; most of the location parameters estimated by the three methods do not differ from each other considerably, while most of the location parameters estimated by MD method are smaller than those estimated by ML method and LS method (in other words, MD method is more conservative); most of the scale parameters estimated by the three methods are close to each other, while almost all the scale parameters estimated by MD method are greater than those estimated by the other two methods (i.e. MD method is more conservative in sense of the degree of dispersion of the estimated Weibull distribution).

Besides, the robustness of MD method is better than the other two methods. Shown in Table 1 are the ranges, the mathematical expectations (the means) and the standard deviations (Std) of the parameters estimated by the different methods for the 30 samples.

# 5. Conclusions

A new parameter estimation method, i.e. the minimum discrepancy method (MD method), is proposed for the three-parameter Weibull distribution. This method is constituted with a minimum discrepancy criterion and a recursion algorithm for parameter searching. By means of a pseudo estimator,  $n$  different scale parameter values are obtained with the individual sample values in a sample of size  $n$ , with given shape parameter and location parameter. The standard deviation of such obtained scale parameter values depends on the shape/location parameter values used in the pseudo estimator. The right shape parameter and location parameter are those minimizing the standard deviation of such estimated scale parameter values. The scale parameter is eventually estimated as the average of the estimated scale parameter values with the individual sample values, based on the right shape parameter and location parameter.

Sixty-five randomly generated samples are used to verify the new parameter estimation method. For the 45 samples from  $W$  (2.0, 1000, 1000), MDE (the estimate of the new method) outperforms MLE (the maximum likelihood estimate) and LSE (the least squares estimate). For the 20 samples from  $W$  (3.0, 1000, 1000), MDE is much better than MLE and LSE. Overall, the new method has advantages in robustness, conservatism, accuracy, and simplicity in the situations of relatively small size of samples ( $n = 15 - 30$ ).
