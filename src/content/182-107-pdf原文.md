# A General Linear-Regression Analysis Applied to the 3-Parameter Weibull Distribution

Yong-Ming Li

No. 5 Research Inst. Ministry of Electronics Industry, Guangzhou

Key Words — Nonlinear regression, Weibull distribution

Reader Aids —

General purpose: Present Weibull and Weibull-like models
Special math needed for explanations: Elementary probability
Special math needed to use results: Same

Results useful to: Reliability analysts & theoreticians

Summary & Conclusions — The conventional techniques of linear regression analysis (linear least squares) applied to the 3-parameter Weibull distribution are extended (not modified), and new techniques are developed for the 3-parameter Weibull distribution. The three pragmatic estimation methods in this paper are simple, accurate, flexible, and powerful in dealing with difficult problems such as estimates of the 3 parameters becoming non-positive. In addition, the inherent disadvantages of the 3-parameter Weibull distribution are revealed; the advantages of a new 3-parameter Weibull-like distribution over the original Weibull distribution are explored; and the potential of a 4-parameter Weibull-like distribution is briefly mentioned.

This paper demonstrates how a general linear regression-analysis or linear least-squares breaks away from the classical or modern nonlinear regression-analysis or nonlinear least-squares. By adding a parameter to the simplest 2-parameter linear regression model (AB-model), two kinds of ABC-models (elementary 3-parameter nonlinear regression models) are found, and then a 4-parameter AABC-model is built as an example of multi-parameter nonlinear regression models. Although some other techniques are still necessary, additional applications of the ABC-models are strongly implied.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Acronyms $^{1}$

AB-model model with $a,b$ as parameters, see (1-5)
ABC-model model with $a,b,c$ as parameters, see (1-6)-(1-7)

AABC-model model with $a_{1}, a_{2}, b, c$ as parameters, see (1-8)
LRA, LLS linear [regression analysis, least squares]
NLRA, NLLS nonlinear [regression analysis, least squares]
GLRA, GLLS general linear [regression analysis, least squares]

2PW, 3PW $_{org}$ [2, 3]-parameter Weibull distribution
3PW $_{new}$ , 4PW [3, 4]-parameter Weibull-like $^{2}$ distribution.

The 2PW Cdf can be transformed into a straight line by a double logarithmic transformation. This allows for a direct application of a LRA or LLS. However, the $3PW_{org}$ Cdf can not be transformed into a straight line by conventional techniques. It seems that no method in the literature tries to apply either classical or modern NLRA or NLLS to the $3PW_{org}$ Cdf, other than the graphical method where the location parameter must be approximated several times. This latter method appears in most books for reliability engineers. Hence, the methods in this paper fill the gap by, for example:

\- improving the graphical method,

\- providing simple, approximate and non-approximate, estimation methods,

\- analyzing the problems of $3\mathrm{PW}_{\mathrm{org}}$ that are ignored by others.

There are many advantages of LRA/LLS & NLRA/NLLS. But the unique one is:

\- a reliability engineer, especially when handling the field data instead of the expensive laboratory data, can simply relocate $^{3}$ those outliers (if there are enough data to do so) without worrying about anything. This advantage is facilitated by the new methods in this paper.

By observing how an additional parameter makes the simplest 2-parameter linear regression model into a nonlinear model, a new $3PW_{new}$ is presented. Compared with this new one, the original $3PW_{org}$ is neither super-accurate nor free from inherent disadvantages; though the new one will never be perfect itself. To improve this situation, the $3PW_{org}$ & $3PW_{new}$ can either be used simultaneously to cover the other's deficiencies or can be combined into 4PW. Numerous papers propose estimation methods and compete with each other for being “even more” accurate.

To search for new pragmatic methods & techniques, this paper proposes a GLRA & GLLS. The ideas of GLRA & GLLS are developed in a forthright way:

\- Add another parameter (to a multi-parameter nonlinear regression model) to open a new area (in applying the simple 2-parameter linear regression model).

A unique advantage is:

\- a reliability engineer does not need to be involved in all of those unfamiliar and/or bothersome nonlinear system problems which can lead to strange solutions.

Though further research has been pursued and many valuable achievements have been made, GLRA & GLLS are still young buds that can grow into a new research branch under the care of reliability analysts & theoreticians.

As is true for other graphical methods, the ones in this paper do not provide any measure of uncertainty in the parameter estimates. Nor are any measures of uncertainty shown for the analytic estimates; such measures could be developed.

The 2PW & 3PW $_{org}$ Cdf's are:

$$
F (t; \alpha , \beta) = 1 - \exp (- t ^ {\beta} / \alpha)\tag{1-1}
$$

$$
F (t; \alpha , \beta , \gamma) = 1 - \exp (- (t - \gamma) ^ {\beta} / \alpha)\tag{1-2}
$$

$$
\text { The   } 3 \mathrm{PW} _ {\text { new }} \& 4 \mathrm{PW} \text {   Cdf's   are: }
$$

$$
F (t; \alpha , \beta , \gamma) = 1 - \exp (- (\mathfrak {t} ^ {\beta} - \gamma) / \alpha)\tag{1-3}
$$

$$
F (t; \alpha , \beta , \gamma_ {1}, \gamma_ {2}) = 1 - \exp (- ((t - \gamma_ {1}) ^ {\beta} - \gamma_ {2}) / \alpha)\tag{1-4}
$$

$$
\alpha > 0; \beta > 0; \gamma , \gamma_ {1}, \gamma_ {2} \geq 0.
$$

The AB-model is:

$$
y = a + b \cdot x + \epsilon\tag{1-5}
$$

The two ABC-models are:

$$
y = (a + b \cdot x) ^ {c} + \epsilon\tag{1-6}
$$

$$
y = a + b \cdot x ^ {c} + \epsilon\tag{1-7}
$$

The AABC-model is:

$$
y = a _ {1} + (a _ {2} + b \cdot x) ^ {c} + \epsilon\tag{1-8}
$$

## 2. TRANSFORMATIONS

The superiority of LRA/LLS is that we can do all kinds of transformations with a strong imagination, and can ignore whether the error $\epsilon$ in a regression model has a s-normal distribution. After a double logarithmic transformation, the 2PW Cdf (1-1) becomes a straight line (1-5), in which:

$$
y = \ln (- \ln (R)), x = \ln (t),\tag{2-1}
$$

$$
a = - \ln (\alpha), b = \beta .
$$

LRA/LLS can be applied to (2-1) directly, eg, Weibull (probability) paper is widely used in reliability practice. This magic really helps us get rid of nonlinear-system problems. The knack is that the calculation of the nonlinear system:

$$
\mathrm{SS} = \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {n} (F _ {i} - (1 - \exp (- t _ {i} ^ {\beta} / \alpha))) ^ {2},\tag{2-2}
$$

$$
\partial \mathrm{SS} / \partial \alpha = \partial \mathrm{SS} / \partial \beta = 0.
$$

is equivalent to the calculation of the linear system:

$$
\mathrm{SS} = \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {n} \left(y _ {i} - (a + b \cdot x _ {i})\right) ^ {2},\tag{2-3}
$$

$$
\partial \mathrm{SS} / \partial a = \partial \mathrm{SS} / \partial b = 0.
$$

SS ≡ sum of squares of deviation from regression line.

The equivalence between (2-2) & (2-3) has a problem, widely studied in statistics concerning “super-accuracy”, but the result is generally acknowledged and beyond the scope of this paper.

In view of the equivalence, the $3PW_{org}$ Cdf (1-2) can be transformed into 2 AB-models.

1. The conventional one (by a double logarithmic transformation):

$y_{1} = a_{1} + b_{1} \cdot x_{1} (\gamma \text{ is assumed known})$ :

$$
y _ {1} = \ln (- \ln (R)), x _ {1} = \ln (t - \gamma),\tag{2-4}
$$

$$
a _ {1} = - \beta \cdot \ln (\alpha), b _ {1} = \beta .
$$

2. A new one (by a single logarithmic transformation):

$$
y _ {2} = a _ {2} + b _ {2} \cdot x _ {2} (\beta \text {   is   assumed   known): }
$$

$$
\begin{array}{l} y _ {2} = (- \ln (R)) ^ {1 / \beta}, x _ {2} = t, \\ a _ {2} = \gamma / \alpha , b _ {2} = 1 / \alpha . \end{array}\tag{2-5}
$$

No evidence shows that (2-4) is better than (2-5). The main reason for preferring (2-4) is probably that (2-4) is similar to (2-1); (2-5) is ignored because of lack of imagination by others. Since the $3PW_{org}$ Cdf can become 2 (instead of 1) straight lines in 2-coordinate systems, LRA/LLS can be applied to it in special ways.

But the $3\mathrm{PW}_{\mathrm{org}}$ Cdf can become 1 (instead of 2) straight line,

$y_{3} = a_{3} + b_{3} \cdot x_{3}$ (no parameter is assumed known):

$$
y _ {3} = R \cdot \ln (f / R), x _ {3} = t,\tag{2-6}
$$

$$
a _ {3} = - \gamma / \beta , b _ {3} = 1 / \beta ,
$$

by a derivation transformation. The value of $f_{i}$ can be estimated from $t_{i}$ & $F_{i}$ by, eg, any of (2-7),

$$
f _ {i, R} \equiv (F _ {i + 1} - F _ {i}) / (t _ {i + 1} - t _ {i}),\tag{2-7a}
$$

$$
f _ {i, L} \equiv (F _ {i} - F _ {i - 1}) / (t _ {i} - t _ {i - 1}), \text { or }\tag{2-7b}
$$

$$
f _ {i} \approx 1 / 2 (f _ {i, R} + f _ {i, L}),\tag{2-7c}
$$

So LRA/LLS can be applied directly to the $3PW_{org}$ Cdf.

The $3PW_{org}$ Cdf can also become another straight line by an integral transformation. But it is not as simple as (2-6); so I prefer (2-6).

Since $\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta}$ can become non-positive (a knotty problem for other estimation methods), (2-4) & (2-5) have their flexible alternatives:

(2-8)

$$
\begin{array}{l} y _ {1} = \ln (- 1 / \ln (R)), x _ {1} = \ln (t - \gamma), \\ a _ {1} = - \beta \cdot \ln (- \alpha), b _ {1} = - \beta . \\ y _ {2} = (- 1 / \ln (R)) ^ {- 1 / \beta}, x _ {2} = - t, \\ a _ {2} = - \gamma / \alpha , b _ {2} = - 1 / \alpha . \end{array}\tag{2-9}
$$

By using (2-4) - (2-5) and (2-8) - (2-9) in turn, the knotty problem can be observed closely and solved easily.

Similarly, imaginative transformations for the $3PW_{new}$ Cdf (1-3), the 4PW Cdf (1-4), and other regression models can also be found. It is always interesting to have a try at them.

## 4. APPROXIMATE ANALYTIC METHOD

Notation

$$
\phi_ {i} \quad [ \overline {{x _ {i} \cdot x _ {i}}} - \overline {{x _ {i}}} ^ {2} ] / [ \overline {{x _ {i} \cdot y _ {i}}} - \overline {{x _ {i}}} \cdot \overline {{y _ {i}}} ].
$$

Since the $3PW_{org}$ Cdf can be a straight line for the approximation (2-7c), a simple formula can be derived from (2-4) & (2-6):

$$
\hat {\gamma} = \overline {{{x _ {3}}}} - \overline {{{y _ {3}}}} \cdot \phi_ {3},\tag{4-1a}
$$

$$
\hat {\beta} = 1 / \phi_ {1},\tag{4-1b}
$$

$$
\ln (\hat {\alpha}) = \overline {{{x _ {1}}}} - \overline {{{y _ {1}}}} \cdot \phi_ {1}.\tag{4-1c}
$$

From my experience, the errors $^{4}$ in the 3 parameter-estimates caused by the approximation (2-9) are about 2% when $n \approx 10$ . The error decreases as n increases. So 2% can be considered as the estimation error of (4-1).

Another simple formula can be derived from (2-5) & (2-6) with the approximation (2-7c):

$$
\hat {\alpha} = \phi_ {2},\tag{4-2a}
$$

$$
\hat {\beta} = 1 / \phi_ {3},\tag{4-2b}
$$

$$
\hat {\gamma} = \overline {{{x _ {2}}}} - \overline {{{y _ {2}}}} \cdot \phi_ {2}.\tag{4-2c}
$$

Though (4-1) might have a little less error than (4-2), eq (4-2) is more convenient in dealing with the knotty problem ( $\hat{\beta} < 0$ ) since $\hat{\beta}$ is estimated first. Relocating suspected outliers until the estimation result becomes satisfactory ( $\hat{\beta} > 0$ ) can be a very useful practical reliability-engineering method, especially when handling field data, since the users can be more or less conscientious.

If less error is required, then I recommend the exact analytic method in section 5.

## 5. EXACT ANALYTIC METHOD

By applying LRA/LLS to the straight line (2-4) in which $\gamma$ is assumed known, $\hat{\beta}$ is obtained, ie, (4-1b) or:

$$
\hat {\beta} = g _ {1} (\gamma).\tag{5-1}
$$

Similarly, to (2-5) in which $\beta$ is assumed known, $\hat{\gamma}$ is obtained, eg, (4-2c) or:

$$
\hat {\gamma} = g _ {2} (\beta).\tag{5-2}
$$

In view of the equivalence, it is easy to understand that the intersection of the two curves (5-1) & (5-2) is the minimum solution. The iteration relationship can be analyzed; eg,

$$
\text { in   (5 - 1)   if } \gamma \rightarrow t _ {\min}, \text { then } g _ {1} (\gamma) \rightarrow - \infty
$$

$$
\text { in   (5 - 2)   if } \beta \rightarrow \infty , \text { then } g _ {2} (\beta) \rightarrow - \infty .
$$

In addition, by applying LRA/LLS to the straight line (2-6), estimates $\hat{\beta}_{R}$ & $\hat{\beta}_{L}$ can be obtained from the approximations (2-7a) & (2-7b) respectively. So the initial iteration-limits are:

$$
\beta_ {\min} = \max (0, \min (\hat {\beta} _ {R}, \hat {\beta} _ {L}))\tag{5-3a}
$$

$$
\beta_ {\max} = \max (0, \hat {\beta} _ {R}, \hat {\beta} _ {L})\tag{5-3b}
$$

$$
\text { Thus } \beta_ {\min} \leq \beta \leq \beta_ {\max}; (\text { see   figure   2 })
$$

$$
\Delta \beta \equiv \beta_ {\max} - \beta_ {\min}\tag{5-4}
$$

![](/182-107-图片/8d138917f27619eec90c2385e64f8bc6c0cbe948a274bd08558e8470b6fd94d6.jpg)
Figure 2. The "Right" & "Left" Approximations

My experience shows that if the curve of the $3PW_{org}$ Cdf is not too sharp, then $\Delta\beta < 2$ when $n \approx 10$ . The $\Delta\beta$ decreases as n increases, so that usually $\Delta\beta \approx \frac{1}{2}$ . The narrow limits on $\beta$ help to avoid the numerical iteration difficulties at multiple kinds of stationary points. I believe that, in practice, only 1 stationary point lies within $\Delta\beta$ .

A simple algorithm is given here (see figure 3).

![](/182-107-图片/92f719a49169b964a7c746cf80e774ebcadbcd584dc8baba09ae7225a851829e.jpg)
Figure 3. A Simple Idea for a Short Computer Program

Algorithm

1. Prepare the two iteration-limits (5-3).

Set $\delta =$ "a small number such as 0.001".

2. Set $\hat{\beta} = \frac{1}{2} (\beta_{\min} + \beta_{\max})$ ; then calculate $\hat{\beta}' = g_1(g_2(\hat{\beta}))$ .

3. If $\hat{\beta}' < \hat{\beta}$ then re-set $\beta_{\min} = \hat{\beta}$ ; else re-set $\beta_{\max} = \hat{\beta}$ . (So that $\Delta \beta$ is reduced rapidly.)

If $\Delta \beta >\delta$ then GoTo step 2.

## 4. Stop

Difficulties

D1. In practice $\Delta\beta$ might large (eg, $\Delta\beta > 5$ ) at the beginning because n is too small, or there is appreciable scatter in the data, or etc. No matter what the reason, $\hat{\beta}$ can still be calculated from the algorithm.

![](/182-107-图片/52c5afaebe96b995e6d87594e593c3fb4c5b39ca5b5f081744903cedc012c8ee.jpg)
Figure 4. Admissible & Inadmissible Solutions

D2. The knotty one wherein $\beta_{min}=\beta_{max}=0$ . The principal reason for this situation is that the data are too censored to describe $3PW_{org}$ reasonably (see figure 4). Very unfortunately, this can rarely be predicted before an estimate has been made. This knotty problem can be quite bothersome for other estimation methods to deal with, since it is one of the inherent disadvantages of $3PW_{org}$ itself. But it can be solved easily here, since a local minimum (for the sum of squares) solution ( $\hat{\beta}>0$ ) can always be found and it is, of course, the optimization of the parameters. For example, the local minimum solution:

$$
\hat {\beta} = 1 0. 0 9, \hat {\alpha} = 1 7. 8 9, \hat {\gamma} = 0. 0 1
$$

can replace the global minimum solution:

$$
\hat {\beta} = - 2, \hat {\alpha} = - 2, \hat {\gamma} = 2 0
$$

very well. To solve this knotty problem more reasonably, I recommend the following pragmatic procedure if there are enough data.

Pragmatic Procedure

1. Find an admissible local minimum solution to replace the inadmissible global minimum solution. This is usually satisfactory.

2. Use the admissible solution and its curve to spot the most-suspect potential outlier (often, the judgment about outliers is very subjective), then relocate it (for the moment).

3. Re-compute to see if the global minimum solution is admissible. If not, then GoTo step 2.

4. Stop.

In addition, I recommend an alternative distribution (see section 6) instead of an evasive estimation method. The alternative seems better in reliability practice (instead of in pure mathematics). Then why not try it in this case.

## 6. $3PW_{new}$ DISTRIBUTION

Two parameters are, sometimes, not enough in a Weibull distribution. Once a location parameter is added to the 2PW, the well-known $3PW_{org}$ is built. But another, unfamiliar one, the $3PW_{new}$ , is also built. Though the latter seems to have been ignored, it has several obvious advantages.

## - Reliability Meanings

Figure 5a. The $3PW_{new}$ pdf vs the 2PW pdf

![](/182-107-图片/14abe071f9ecc62f5b55f15275a5cc85ac8fd4e09df1ac67bb1b3c3610cd0471.jpg)
Figure 5b. The $3PW_{org}$ pdf vs the 2PW pdf

According to $3PW_{new}$ , we know that, eg, the electronic components or parts which would fail during the time 0 < t < $\hat{\gamma}^{1/\bar{\beta}}$ in a life test have been screened out by efficient reliability controls (see figure 5a). Whereas according to $3PW_{org}$ , it seems that, eg, a life test which should start at time t=0 (say, 8:00 am) as if started at another time $t=\hat{\gamma}$ (say, 8:00 pm) by a mistake (see figure 5b).

## • Mathematical Characters

In $3PW_{new}$ , t > 0 is always required even if $\hat{\gamma} < 0$ (see figure 6a). Whereas in the $3PW_{org}$ function, t < 0 is never restricted (see figure 6b); no matter what estimation methods can be developed, the problem t < 0 will never be solved.

## - Smoothing Similarity

Figure 6a. The $3PW_{new}$ Cdf vs the 2PW Cdf

The $3PW_{new}$ Cdf is quite similar to the $3PW_{org}$ Cdf (see figure 7), and both are exactly the same when $\hat{\gamma}=0$ or $\hat{\beta}=1$ . The difference between the two Cdf's is usually much smaller than the errors in practical data (especially, in field data). So $3PW_{new}$ can be used any place where $3PW_{org}$ is well used, and even replace the $3PW_{org}$ when it “fails”.

## • Estimation Convenience

Because the estimation methods for the $3PW_{new}$ are extremely similar to those for the $3PW_{org}$ (the graphic method is an example given above), and because the $3PW_{new}$ pdf is exactly the same as the 2PW pdf except for a proportional-constant, much estimation convenience can be anticipated.

## • Distribution Advancement

The $3PW_{new}$ can be combined with $3PW_{org}$ to form a robust 4PW with 2 location parameters. The old “unsolvable” problem, $\hat{\gamma} < 0$ , in $3PW_{org}$ can now be solved by replacing $3PW_{org}$ with 4PW in which, $\hat{\gamma}_{2} = (-\hat{\gamma}_{1})^{\hat{\beta}}$ since those electronic components or parts which would fail during $\hat{\gamma} < t < 0$ or $-\infty < t < 0$ have certainly been screened-out before a life test (see figure 8).

## 7. ABC-MODELS

There are many kinds of 2-parameter nonlinear regression models such as $y = a \cdot x + b$ , or $y = a + \ln(1 + b \cdot x)$ . All of them can be transformed into the AB-model to which LRA/LLS can be applied directly. Similarly, all kinds of 3-parameter nonlinear regression models can be transformed into the 2 ABC-models, to which GLRA/GLLS can be applied identically. The $3\mathrm{PW}_{\mathrm{org}}$ Cdf is still used as the example.

Algorithm

1. Transform the $3PW_{org}$ Cdf (1-2) into the ABC-model (1-6):

$$
y = - \ln (1 - F), x = t;\tag{7-1a}
$$

$$
a = - \gamma / \alpha , b = 1 / \alpha , c = \beta .\tag{7-1b}
$$

2a. Transform the ABC-model into 2 AB-models:

$$
y _ {1} = \ln (y), x _ {1} = \ln (x + a / b),\tag{7-2a}
$$

$$
a _ {1} = c \cdot \ln (b), b _ {1} = c;\tag{7-2b}
$$

$$
y _ {2} = y ^ {1 / c}, x _ {2} = x,\tag{7-3a}
$$

$$
a _ {2} = a, b _ {2} = b;\tag{7-3b}
$$

2b. Transform the ABC-model into a real AB-model:

$$
y _ {3} = y / y ^ {\prime}, x _ {3} = x,\tag{7-4a}
$$

$$
a _ {3} = a / (b \cdot c), b _ {3} = 1 / c.\tag{7-4b}
$$

3. Obtain the data $y_{i}^{\prime}$ from the data $x_{i}$ & $y_{i}$ by:

$$
y _ {i, R} ^ {\prime} \equiv (y _ {i + 1} - y _ {i}) / (x _ {i + 1} - x _ {i}),\tag{7-5a}
$$

$$
y _ {i, L} ^ {\prime} \equiv (y _ {i} - y _ {i - 1}) / (x _ {i} - x _ {i - 1}), \text { or }\tag{7-5b}
$$

$$
y _ {i} ^ {\prime} \approx \frac {1}{2} (y _ {i, R} ^ {\prime} + y _ {i, L} ^ {\prime}).\tag{7-5c}
$$

4. Obtain $\hat{c}_{R}$ with (7-5a), and $\hat{c}_{L}$ with (7-5b). Set up the initial iteration-limits:

$$
c _ {\min} = \max (0, \min (\hat {c} _ {R}, \hat {c} _ {L})),\tag{7-6a}
$$

$$
c _ {\max} = \max (0, \hat {c} _ {R}, \hat {c} _ {L});\tag{7-6b}
$$

$$
\text { thus } \hat {c} _ {\min} <   \hat {c} <   \hat {c} _ {\max}.\tag{7-7}
$$

If $c_{\min} = c_{\max} = 0$ then put $y = 1 / y, x = -x$ .

5. Estimate $\hat{a}, \hat{b}, \hat{c}$ by one of the 3 methods; then —

$$
\hat {\alpha} = 1 / \hat {b}, \hat {\beta} = \hat {c}, \hat {\gamma} = - \hat {a} / \hat {b}.
$$

This algorithm summarizes the contents of this paper.

Similarly, the $3PW_{new}$ Cdf (1-3) can be transformed into the other ABC-model (1-7).

The 2 ABC-models are 2 elementary 3-parameter nonlinear regression models. They can be used to form large multiparameter nonlinear regression models. For example, the AABC-model (1-8) is directly built on the 2 ABC-models, ie, it will be (1-6) when $a_{1}=0$ ; and (1-7) when $a_{2}=0$ . So it can be analyzed based upon the analyses of the 2 ABC-models, though a few other techniques are still necessary.
