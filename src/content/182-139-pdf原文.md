# Properties of Moment Estimators For the 3-Parameter Weibull Distribution

Martin Newby
University of Bradford, Bradford

Key Words—Weibull distribution; Moment estimator; s-Confidence limit.

Reader Aids—

Purpose: Widen state of the art

Special math needed for explanations: Statistics, Gamma function, Digamma function

Special math needed to use results: Statistics

Results useful to: Reliability theoreticians and statisticians

Abstract—This paper gives a method of constructing moment estimators for the shape, scale, and location parameters of the 3-parameter Weibull distribution. These estimators are asymptotically s-normally distributed around the population values; the asymptotic covariance matrix is obtained. The estimators and their estimated covariance matrix can be constructed by using tables in the paper; the covariance matrix can then be used to construct s-confidence regions for the estimators.

## INTRODUCTION

Refs [1, 2] show that moment estimators for the 2-parameter Weibull distribution are asymptotically s-normally distributed and give a table for estimating the parameters and their covariance matrix. This paper extends that work to 3-parameter Weibull distribution. Moment estimators are useful in the 3-parameter case because the s-information matrix of the maximum-likelihood estimators has singularities when the shape parameter is less than or equal to 2, and thus cannot be inverted to estimate the covariance matrix of the estimators. Further, the singularities mean that the use of an iterative method to solve the likelihood equations is likely to be unstable if it uses the second derivatives (the Newton-Raphson method, for example) of the log-likelihood function. However, the asymptotic covariance matrix of the moment estimators exists for all values of the shape parameter. Saylor [3] has shown, using simulation, in two particular cases with shape parameter less than 2 that the moment estimators are more s-efficient than maximum-likelihood estimators, and that their performance relative to the maximum-likelihood estimators is better for small samples than for large samples.

## THE SHAPE, SCALE, AND LOCATION PARAMETER FAMILY

The results are obtained by exploiting the relation between the moments of the parent distribution with $Cdf\{t; k\}$ , $0 \leq t < \infty$ , and the derived distribution $Cdf\{(t - c)/b; k\}$ . The moments of the parent distribution are functions of k alone. The moments of the two distributions then satisfy:

$$
M _ {1} = c + b m _ {1};
$$

$$
v _ {j} = b ^ {j} u _ {j}.\tag{1a}
$$

(1b)

Now define the standardised moments:

$$
R _ {j} (k) = v _ {j} / v _ {2} ^ {j / 2} = u _ {j} / u _ {2} ^ {j / 2}, \text {   for   } j \geq 3.\tag{2a}
$$

The $R_{j}$ are functions of k alone. For a sample of size n write the standardized sample moments as:

$$
r _ {j} = a _ {j} / a _ {2} ^ {j / 2}.\tag{2b}
$$

The estimators for the shape, scale, and location, are now defined by:

$$
\begin{array}{r l} R _ {j} (k ^ {*}) & = r _ {j}; \\ b ^ {*} & = [ a _ {2} / u _ {2} (k ^ {*}) ] ^ {1 / 2}; \\ c ^ {*} & = a _ {1} - b ^ {*} m _ {1} (k ^ {*}). \end{array}\tag{2c}
$$

In (2), $u_{2}(k^{*})$ and $m_{1}(k^{*})$ are the variance and the mean of the parent population, assuming that $k = k^{*}$ . Dubey [4-6] has investigated the properties of estimators for the location and scale parameters of the Weibull when the shape is known and has studied the properties of some simple estimators for the shape parameter; here the estimators for shape, scale, and location are treated jointly.

Following the arguments outlined by Kendall & Stuart [7, chapter 10] the first order Taylor expansions of the estimators around their population values can be used to show that:

$$
\operatorname{Var} \{k ^ {*} \} \cong A ^ {2} \cdot \operatorname{Var} \{r _ {j} \};\tag{3a}
$$

$$
\operatorname{Cov} \left\{k ^ {*}, b ^ {*} \right\} \cong \mathrm{AP} \cdot \operatorname{Cov} \left\{a _ {2}, r _ {j} \right\} + A ^ {2} Q \cdot \operatorname{Var} \left\{r _ {j} \right\};\tag{3b}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r l} \operatorname{Cov} \left\{k ^ {*}, c ^ {*} \right\} & \cong A \cdot \operatorname{Cov} \left\{a _ {1}, r _ {j} \right\} + A B \cdot \operatorname{Cov} \left\{a _ {2}, r _ {j} \right\} \\ & + A ^ {2} C \cdot \operatorname{Var} \left\{r _ {j} \right\}; \end{array}\tag{3c}
$$

$$
\operatorname{Var} \left\{b ^ {*} \right\} \cong P ^ {2} \cdot \operatorname{Var} \left\{a _ {2} \right\} + (A Q) ^ {2} \cdot \operatorname{Var} \left\{r _ {j} \right\}
$$

$$
+ 2 A P Q \cdot \operatorname{Cov} \left\{a _ {2}, r _ {j} \right\};\tag{3d}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r l} \operatorname{Cov} \{b ^ {*}, c ^ {*} \} & \cong P \cdot \operatorname{Cov} \{a _ {1}, a _ {2} \} + P B \cdot \operatorname{Var} \{a _ {2} \} \\ & + (A P C + A B Q) \cdot \operatorname{Cov} \{a _ {2}, r _ {j} \} \\ & + A Q \operatorname{Cov} \{a _ {1}, r _ {j} \} + A ^ {2} C Q \cdot \operatorname{Var} \{r _ {j} \}; \end{array}\tag{3e}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r l} \operatorname{Var} \{c ^ {*} \} & \cong \operatorname{Var} \{a _ {1} \} + B ^ {2} \cdot \operatorname{Var} \{a _ {2} \} + (A C) ^ {2} \cdot \operatorname{Var} \{r _ {j} \} \\ & + 2 (B \cdot \operatorname{Cov} \{a _ {1}, a _ {2} \} + A B C \cdot \operatorname{Cov} \{a _ {2}, r _ {j} \}) \\ & + A C \cdot \operatorname{Cov} \{a _ {1}, r _ {j} \}). \end{array}\tag{3f}
$$

The coefficients $A, B, C, P, Q$ are:

$$
\mathrm{A} ^ {- 1} \equiv R _ {j} \left\{\frac {1}{u _ {j} (k ^ {*})} \cdot \frac {d u _ {j}}{d k ^ {*}} - \frac {j}{2 u _ {2} (k ^ {*})} \cdot \frac {d u _ {2}}{d k ^ {*}} \right\} = \frac {d R _ {j}}{d k ^ {*}};\tag{4a}
$$

$$
B \equiv - m _ {1} (k ^ {*}) / [ 2 b u _ {2} (k ^ {*}) ];\tag{4b}
$$

$$
C \equiv b \left\{\frac {m}{2 u _ {2} (k ^ {*})} \cdot \frac {d u _ {2}}{d k ^ {*}} - \frac {d m _ {1}}{d k ^ {*}} \right\};\tag{4c}
$$

$$
P ^ {- 1} \equiv 2 b u _ {2} (k ^ {*});\tag{4d}
$$

$$
Q \equiv \frac {- 1}{2 u _ {2} (k ^ {*})} \cdot \frac {d u _ {2}}{d k ^ {*}};\tag{4e}
$$

where all functions and derivatives are evaluated at $k^{*} = k$ . The asymptotic s-normality of the distribution of the estimators now follows from Cramér [8, theorem 28.4]. Details of the calculations of $\operatorname{Var}\{a_{1}\}$ , $\operatorname{Cov}\{a_{1}, r_{j}\}$ , etc. can be found in the appendix.

By arguing as before [1], the asymptotic covariance matrix is:

$$
\mathbf {V} = \quad \operatorname{Var} \left\{k ^ {*} \right\} \quad \operatorname{Cov} \left\{k ^ {*}, b ^ {*} \right\} \quad \operatorname{Cov} \left\{k ^ {*}, c ^ {*} \right\}\tag{5}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r l r l r l} & = (1 / n) \quad 1 0 0 & h _ {1 1} h _ {1 2} h _ {1 3} & 1 0 0 \\ & 0 b 0 & h _ {1 2} h _ {2 2} h _ {2 3} & 0 b 0 \\ & 0 0 b & h _ {1 3} h _ {2 3} h _ {3 3} & 0 0 b \\ & = (1 / n) \mathbf {T H T} \end{array}
$$

which shows that V is obtained from a standardised covariance matrix by a scale transformation.

## ESTIMATORS FOR THE 3-PARAMETER WEIBULL

The parent $Cdf\{t; k\} = \text{weif}(t; k)$ and the derived $Cdf\{t; k, b, c\} = \text{weif}((t - c)/b; k)$ . The estimators are obtained by using the sample-moment skewness, $r_{3}$ . The non-central moments and their derivatives for the standard Weibull are:

$$
m _ {j} = \Gamma (1 + j / k)
$$

$$
\frac {d m _ {j}}{d k} = - j m _ {j} \psi (1 + j / k) / k ^ {2}.
$$

The functions $h_{ij}$ can now be evaluated. The standardised mean, variance, and moment skewness are tabulated together with the $h_{ij}$ in table 1. The gamma function is evaluated by using a reduction formula and an asymptotic expansion [9, formulas 6.1.15 & 6.1.41] and the digamma function is treated in the same way [9, formulas 6.3.6 & 6.3.18]. It is possible for $c^{*}$ to exceed the smallest observation and Dubey [6] has suggested an alternative estimator,

$$
\hat {c} = t _ {\min} - b ^ {*} m _ {1} (k ^ {*}) / n ^ {1 / k ^ {*}},
$$

obtained from the s-expected value of the minimum of a sample of size n. When $\hat{c}$ is used then the above results do not apply, but its variance is given in [6], and table 1 can still be used to estimate the variances of the estimates of the shape and scale parameters.

## EXAMPLE

The data in table 2 are an ordered sample from a Weibull distribution with k = 2, b = 20, and c = 10 generated from [10]. The sample size is 25, the sample moments are $a_{1} = 25.12$ , $a_{2} = 69.84$ , $a_{3} = 378.51$ , $r_{3} = 0.6485$ . Linear interpolation in table 1 shows that $k^{*} = 1.98$ , $b^{*} = 17.81$ , $c^{*} = 9.33$ . Linear interpolation in table 1 gives good agreement with solutions of (2c) obtained by the method of repeated bisection. Linear interpolation is also used to obtain the following estimates of the elements of the covariance matrix:

$$
\begin{array}{c} \operatorname{Var} \{k ^ {*} \} = 1 3. 9 5 0 2 / 2 5 = 0. 5 6; \\ \operatorname{Cov} \{k ^ {*}, b ^ {*} \} = 1 7. 8 1 (5. 4 8 8) / 2 5 = 3. 9 1; \\ \operatorname{Cov} \{k ^ {*}, c ^ {*} \} = 1 7. 8 1 (- 4. 4 8 9 8) / 2 5 = - 3. 2 0; \\ \operatorname{Var} \{b ^ {*} \} = (1 7. 8 1) ^ {2} (2. 6 5 9 1) / 2 5 = 3 3. 7 4; \\ \operatorname{Cov} \{b ^ {*}, c ^ {*} \} = (1 7. 8 1) ^ {2} (- 2. 0 3 8 3) / 2 5 = - 2 5. 8 6; \\ \operatorname{Var} \{c ^ {*} \} = (1 7. 8 1) ^ {2} (1. 7 5 0 5) / 2 5 = 2 2. 2 1. \end{array}
$$

## ASYMPTOTIC s-EFFICIENCY OF THE ESTIMATORS

The asymptotic information matrix for the maximum likelihood estimators of $k, b, c$ for a sample of size $n$ is $\mathbf{M} = -n\mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{T}^{-1}$ . $\mathbf{G} \equiv (g_{ij})$ where —

$$
g _ {1 1} \equiv - (1 + \Gamma^ {\prime \prime} (2)) / k,\tag{6a}
$$

$$
g _ {1 2} \equiv \Gamma^ {\prime} (2),\tag{6b}
$$

$$
g _ {1 3} \equiv (1 - 1 / k) \Gamma (1 - 1 / k) \{\psi (1 - 1 / k) + 1 \},
$$

$$
g _ {2 2} \equiv - k ^ {2},\tag{6c}
$$

$$
g _ {2 3} \equiv - k (k - 1) \Gamma (1 - 1 / k),\tag{6d}
$$

$$
g _ {3 3} \equiv - (k - 1) \Gamma (1 - 2 / k).\tag{6e}
$$

(6f)

For details of the calculation of the $g_{ij}$ see [1, 4].

Following Geary [11] the generalised variance of the moment estimators is $V_{1} = \det(\mathbf{V})$ and that of the maximum likelihood estimators is $M_{1} = \det(\mathbf{M}^{-1})$ . The joint asymptotic s-efficiency is [11-13]:

$$
E = M 1 / V 1 = - 1 / [ \det (\mathbf {G}) \det (\mathbf {H}) ].
$$

The s-efficiency, E, is a function of k alone, and is tabulated against k in table 3. The elements $g_{13}$ , $g_{23}$ , and $g_{33}$ of G have singularities for $k \leq 2$ , so that the s-efficiency exists only for k > 2. For k near 2 the s-efficiency is poor, but for other values of k it is reasonable. Listings of the computer programs can be obtained from the author, or from the supplement [14]. Saylor has shown empirically [3] that the moment estimators are more s-efficient than the maximum-likelihood estimators when $k \leq 2$ .

## APPENDIX

The methodology for determining approximate values of variances and covariances is given by Kendall & Stuart [7] and is straightforward but tedious. The required variances and covariances are:

$$
\begin{array}{r l} \operatorname{Var} \left\{r _ {j} \right\} & \cong R _ {j} ^ {2} \left[ \operatorname{Var} \left\{a _ {j} \right\} / v _ {j} ^ {2} + j ^ {2} \operatorname{Var} \left\{a _ {2} \right\} / \left(4 v _ {2} ^ {2}\right) \right. \\ & \left. - j \operatorname{Cov} \left\{a _ {2}, a _ {j} \right\} / \left(v _ {2} v _ {j}\right) \right]; \end{array}
$$

n. $\operatorname{Var}\{a_1\} = b^2 u_2$ ;

n. $\operatorname{Var}\{a_2\} = b^4 (u_4 - u_2^2)$ ;

$$
\mathrm{n.Cov} \left\{a _ {1}, r _ {j} \right\} \cong R _ {j} \left[ \operatorname{Cov} \left\{a _ {1}, a _ {j} \right\} / v _ {j} - j \operatorname{Cov} \left\{a _ {1}, a _ {2} \right\} / (2 v _ {2}) \right];
$$

n. $\operatorname{Cov}\{a_2, r_j\} \cong R_j[\operatorname{Cov}\{a_2, a_j\} / v_j - j \operatorname{Var}\{a_2\} / (2v_2)]$ ;

n. $\operatorname{Cov}\{a_2, a_j\} \cong v_{j+2} - v_j v_2 - jv_{j-1}v_3;$

n. $\operatorname{Cov}\{a, a\} \cong v_{j+1} - jv_{j-1}v_2$ .
