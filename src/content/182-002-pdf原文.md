## Abstract

The lifetime data collected from the field are usually heavily censored, in which case, getting an accurate reliability evaluation based on heavily censored data is challenging. For heavily Type-II censored data, the parameters estimation bias of traditional methods (i.e., maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) and least squares estimation (LSE)) are still large, and Bayesian methods are hard to specify the priors in practice. Therefore, considering the existing range of shape parameter for Weibull distribution, this study proposes two novel parameter estimation methods, the three-step MLE method and the hybrid estimation method. For the three-step MLE method, the initial estimates of shape and scale parameters are first respectively derived using MLE, then are updated by the single parameter MLE method with the range constraint of shape parameter. For the hybrid estimation method, the shape parameter is estimated by the LSE method with the existing range constraint of shape parameter, then the scale parameter estimate can be obtained by MLE. On this basis, two numerical examples are performed to demonstrate the consistency and effectiveness of the proposed methods. Finally, a case study on turbine engines is given to verify the effectiveness and applicability of the proposed methods.

## 1 INTRODUCTION

With the rapid advancement of science and technology, products with high reliability and long life have emerged, making it hard to obtain sufficient lifetime data while considering test costs and time.1,2 In addition, the lifetime data collected from the field are usually heavily censored,3,4 and the censored proportion of collected data is often greater than 0.5. Those data are called heavily censored data, which always lack enough information. Therefore, it is a heavy challenge to get an accurate reliability evaluation from the heavily censored failure data.

Several statistical distributions, including Log-normal distribution, Exponential distribution, and Weibull distribution, are often used to describe the lifetime data of products. Among them, the Weibull distribution, defined by shape and scale parameters, is widely used as a model with diverse types of items, such as ball bearings and automobile components.5 Besides, the Type-II censored data are often obtained in practice, where the test will be terminated at a pre-fixed failure number ??. For the heavily Type-II censored data, the classical parameter estimation methods, such as maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) and least squares estimation (LSE), cannot provide stable and precise estimates.6,7

Therefore, some efforts have been made to deal with the estimation problem of heavily censored data. Several researchers have proposed novel estimation methods based on MLE. Under the adaptive progressive Type-II censored data, Yan et al.8 adopted the MLE method and the Bootstrap method to assess product reliability. Additionally, for the generalized Type-I censored data, Starling et al.9 combined a modified MLE with an oversampling technique to estimate Weibull parameters. Through extensive simulations and comparative studies, Yang et al.10 compared the estimation performance of various methods and gave their recommendations under small samples. In addition, Zhu et al.11 proposed a new Newton−Raphson algorithm to compute the MLE estimations under the modified progressive hybrid censored data. For the generalized progressive hybrid censored data, Maswadah et al.12 proposed an improved MLE method using the Runge–Kutta technique. For Type-I censored data, Makalic et al.13 developed a new bias-adjusted MLE method to improve the estimate accuracy of shape parameter. Besides, to solve the estimation problem of scale parameter, Kumar et al.14 combined the generalized Pareto distribution and the MLE method to obtain a better scale parameter estimate. The above previous studies are mainly on MLE, while there are several studies on LSE. For instance, Zhang et al.15 found that the estimation bias of shape parameter can be modeled as a function of sample size and censoring degree, and they proposed a reduced bias function for complete and censored data. Jiang16 proposed a new data augmentation method and combined it with LSE to give point estimates of distribution parameters. The above methods outperform the classical ones; however, they ignore the additional failure information, and cannot yield satisfactory reliability evaluation results for heavily censored data.17

To further enhance the parameter estimation accuracy, previous studies have attempted to extract and utilize the additional failure information. For example, Ducros et al.18 combined the Bayesian method with the Bootstrap approach to solve the parameter estimation problem for a two-component Weibull mixture under heavily censored data. For the Type-II censored data, Algarni et al.19 presented a novel Bayesian method by utilizing the squared error loss function and the gamma distribution as the conjugate prior distribution of unknown parameters. Meanwhile, some researchers combined the Bayesian method with LSE,20 MLE,17 or other techniques21,22 to reduce the estimation bias. In addition, Liu et al.23 proposed a novel method for parameter estimation under the Type-I censored data, which combines the Bayesian and weighted least squares methods. Given the shape parameter value as the prior information, Zhang et al.24,25 developed two estimation methods based on the Bayesian approach for the zero-failure data. They indicated that the failure time data hold much useful information and have a significant impact on the estimation of shape parameter for Weibull distribution. Thus, those parameter estimation methods based on the Bayesian approach have a good performance for heavily censored data. However, they are hard to specify the priors and lack operability,25,26 which cannot yield satisfactory reliability evaluation results.

To solve the above problems, some studies have proposed non-traditional methods, which utilize the characteristic of failure data to extract additional information. Considering the two-step single-parameter MLE (SP-MLE) method and the characteristics of known heavily censored datasets, Jiang et al.6,27 proposed two novel methods to estimate parameters and mean time to failure (MTTF) for Weibull distribution. These methods gave an insight into the reliability evaluation with randomly and heavily censored data, which generally involve two steps. The first step is to estimate MTTF by utilizing the information from the collected failure data, such as using an experience-based method (EBM)6 or a partly experience-based method (PEBM).27 Then, with the estimate of MTTF, the shape parameter is estimated by SP-MLE. Once the estimate of shape parameter is specified, the scale parameter is also calculated by SP-MLE. For the randomly and heavily censored data, parameter estimates from the two methods are much more accurate than those from MLE and LSE. However, as illustrated in Subsection 2.2, they are not consistent for Type II censored data.

In summary, for the heavily censored Type-II data, there are three drawbacks in the existing works: (1) the estimation biases of parameters obtained by MLE and LSE are still large, (2) Bayesian methods are hard to specify the priors in practice, (3) the non-traditional methods from the failure data are not consistent for Type-II censored data. Hence, further studies are still needed. Through the above analysis on parameter estimation for heavily censored data, the utilization of additional information is essential to improve the estimation accuracy of distribution parameters. Therefore, an easier way should be explored first to utilize additional useful information for getting better estimates of distribution parameters.

For products following a Weibull distribution, it is well-known that the shape parameter corresponds to the failure mechanism, which is very important to obtain an accurate estimate of the shape parameter. For products following a Weibull distribution, when the shape parameter is within the range of zero and one, the products are in early failures, while the Weibull distribution is equivalent to an exponential distribution when the shape parameter is equal to one. In addition, when the shape parameter is bigger than one, the products tend to be in wear-out failures, which are associated with damage accumulation processes, and typically attributed to wear, tear, and aging of the equipment. Therefore, based on products in wear-out failures, the parameter for characterizing failure mechanism should be taken into consideration to obtain more accurate parameter estimation results for Weibull distribution.

Several studies have discussed the shape parameter range of Weibull distribution for different products. For example, Jiang28 indicated that the interval of shape parameter can be larger than 2 and rarely larger than 4 for well-designed and manufactured products. As another example, a reasonable range of shape parameter for the lifetime of rotorcraft composite materials is from 1.84 to 2.891.29 Yang et al.30 explored the range of shape parameter for describing the brittle failure behavior of rocks, and they investigated the shape parameter should be larger than 1 and lower than 6. In general, it is feasible to specify a valuable range of shape parameter for Weibull distribution by certain means. These means involve but are not limited to (1) historical data or available data from similar products,31,32 (2) the expert information based on extensive engineering theory and experience to specify an accurate range of shape parameter.33,34 Hence, an accurate range of shape parameter can significantly contribute to obtaining stable and accurate parameter estimates for heavily censored data. Besides, as mentioned above, the existing methods are mostly based on MLE and LSE or their variants. Therefore, based on the obtained range constraint of shape parameter in practice, two novel estimation methods are proposed in this study for heavily censored Type-II data by introducing additional useful information into MLE and LSE, respectively.

The main contributions of this study are as follows.

2. Considering the existing range of shape parameter, the constrained least squares estimation is used to estimate the shape parameter, and MLE is adopted to estimate the scale parameter, thereby, a novel hybrid estimation method is obtained for heavily censored Type-II data.
3. The good statistical properties of the two proposed methods are investigated through simulations.

## 2 RELATED WORK FOR HEAVILY CENSORED DATA

This section will introduce the description of heavily censored data first, and briefly review some of the past and current methods commonly used for parameter estimation, then evaluate their performance based on heavily Type-II censored data.

## 2.1 Heavily censored data

Supposed that there is a random sample of ?? units and it is chosen for testing with ?? failure terminated data $t _ { 1 } , t _ { 2 } , \ldots , t _ { r } .$ where $r \leq n$ . For the heavily censored data, there is the censoring degree $c = ~ n - r ) / n \geq 0 . 5$ .

Weibull distribution is a widely applied distribution in reliability engineering, which is commonly used to describe lifetime data.35–37 Assume that the product lifetime follows a Weibull distribution, its cumulative distribution function (CDF) is defined by

$$
F (t) = 1 - \exp \left[ - \left(\frac {t}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta} \right], t \geq 0, \tag {1}
$$

and its probability density function (PDF) is

$$
f (t) = \frac {\beta t ^ {\beta - 1}}{\eta^ {\beta}} \exp \left[ - \left(\frac {t}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta} \right], t \geq 0, \tag {2}
$$

where $\beta > 0$ is the shape parameter, and $\eta > 0$ is the scale parameter.

## 3 PROPOSED PARAMETER ESTIMATION METHODS

As pointed out in Section 2, the additional useful information is essential to improve the estimation accuracy of parameters $\beta , \ \eta .$ , and $\mu$ for heavily censored data. In addition, getting a valuable range of the failure mechanism characterization parameter is more feasible than other additional useful information, since this parameter can characterize the failure information for products.

For a Weibull distribution, shape parameter $\beta$ can characterize the failure mechanism for products. Thus, through existing theoretical study and engineering experience in practice, it is feasible to obtain a reliable range of $\beta$ for specific products. In general, the constraint form for $\beta$ is given by

$$
\beta_ {m i n} \leq \beta \leq \beta_ {m a x},
$$

where $\beta _ { m i n } \geq 1$ , since $\beta$ is always bigger than 1 for wear-out failures.

As mentioned in Subsection 2.2.2, the estimation errors of $\beta$ show different patterns under different sample sizes ?? and censoring degree $^ { c , }$ for example, with $n = 3 0$ and $c = 0 . 6 $ , estimated errors of $\beta$ from MLE are less than those from LSE, while ones from LSE are less than from MLE when $n = 1 0$ and $c = 0 . 6$ . Therefore, the characteristics of MLE and LSE should be considered in this study.

In summary, considering the characteristics of MLE and LSE, this section will propose two consistent estimation methods with the existing range of shape parameter as the additional information to improve the parameter estimation accuracy.

## 3.1 A novel three-step MLE with shape parameter constraint

Considering the range of shape parameter as the additional useful information, we propose a novel three-step MLE with shape parameter constraint (CMLE) based on PEBM to obtain accurate parameter estimates. The detailed steps are as follows.

First, the initial estimates of $\beta$ and $\eta$ (denoted as $\beta _ { C 0 }$ and $\eta _ { C 0 } .$ , respectively) are obtained by MLE. Thereby the initial estimate of $\mu$ (denoted as $\mu _ { C 0 } )$ can be calculated by

$$
\mu_ {C 0} = \eta_ {C 0} \Gamma \left(1 + \frac {1}{\beta_ {C 0}}\right). \tag {14}
$$

Second, put an exact constraint on $\beta$ to obtain accurate estimates of parameters $\beta , \eta$ , and $\mu .$ With the estimated $\eta _ { C 0 } ,$ , the estimate of $\beta ( \mathrm { i } . \mathrm { e } . , \beta _ { C } )$ is updated by the following linear programming program,

$$
\max r \ln \beta - r \ln \eta + (\beta - 1) \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {r} \ln t _ {i} - \frac {1}{\beta \sum^ {*} t _ {i} ^ {\beta}}, \tag {15}
$$

$$
s. t. 1 \leq \beta_ {m i n} \leq \beta \leq \beta_ {m a x}.
$$

where $\begin{array} { r } { \eta = \eta _ { C 0 } , \sum ^ { * } t _ { i } ^ { \beta } = \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { r } t _ { i } ^ { \beta } + ( n - r ) t _ { r } ^ { \beta } . } \end{array}$

Thirdly, with fixed ????, SP-MLE is used to re-estimate $\eta$ (denoted as $\eta _ { C } )$ with the following function,

$$

$$

## 3.2 A hybrid estimation method with shape parameter constraints $\mathbf { c } = \mathbf { 0 . 6 }$

Considering the existing range of $\beta ,$ we propose a hybrid estimation method (HEM) to improve the estimation accuracy of parameters under the heavily Type-II censored data. The detailed steps are as follows.

First, LSE with the existing limit $[ \beta _ { m i n } , \beta _ { m a x } ]$ is given by the following nonlinear programming program,

$$
\max \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {n} \left(y _ {i} ^ {\prime} - \left(\hat {a} + \hat {\beta} x _ {i}\right)\right) ^ {2}, \tag {18}
$$

$$
s. t. 1 \leq \beta_ {m i n} \leq \beta \leq \beta_ {m a x}.
$$

Thus, the estimate of $\beta$ (denoted as $\beta _ { H } )$ is calculated as $\beta _ { H } = b .$ .

Second, a precise estimate of $\dot { \eta }$ can be obtained by MLE. Taking the derivatives of $\beta$ and $\eta$ on both sides of Equation (3) and making the derivatives equal to zero, we can obtain the following two equations,

$$
\frac {\partial l n L}{\partial \beta} = \frac {\sum_ {i = 1} ^ {r} t _ {i} ^ {\beta} l n t _ {i} + (n - r) t _ {r} ^ {\beta} l n t _ {r}}{\sum_ {i = 1} ^ {r} t _ {i} ^ {\beta} + (n - r) t _ {r} ^ {\beta}} - \frac {1}{\beta} - \frac {1}{r} \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {r} l n t _ {i}, \tag {19}
$$

$$
\eta^ {\beta} = \frac {\sum_ {i = 1} ^ {r} t _ {i} ^ {\beta} + (n - r) t _ {r} ^ {\beta}}{r}, \tag {20}
$$

and the estimate of $\eta$ (denoted as $\eta _ { H } )$ is calculated by Equation (20).

Third, the estimate of $\mu$ (denoted as $\mu _ { H } )$ can be calculated by the following,

$$
\mu_ {H} = \eta_ {H} \Gamma \left(1 + \frac {1}{\beta_ {H}}\right). \tag {21}

Finally, the final estimates of ${ \vec { \beta } } , \eta ,$ , and $\mu ( \mathrm { i . e . , } \beta _ { H } , \eta _ { H }$ , and $\mu _ { H } )$ are obtained by the proposed HEM method.

## 5 CASE STUDY

To verify the effectiveness and applicability of the proposed methods, a real case study on turbine engines is presented in this section. Turbine engines are widely used in rotating machinery such as helicopters, auxiliary power units, and hovercrafts. The structure of a turbine engine mainly consists of three core components: the compressor, the combustion chamber, and the turbine. Nowadays, turbine engines have become the main power source of large airliners, fighter planes, helicopters, and other flying machines. However, the working condition of turbine engines directly affects the performance of aircraft. Since the aircraft always stays in the air for a long time, its failure is immeasurable no matter personal safety or economic loss. Therefore, to obtain accurate life for turbine engines, we conducted a life test on 50 engines under a Type-II censoring scheme and then collected their life data, which are shown in Table 9.

## 6 CONCLUSIONS

Considering the failure mechanism characterization parameter for highly reliable products, this study presents two novel reliability evaluation methods, CMLE and HEM, for the heavily Type-II censored data, respectively. As well known, a relatively accurate interval of shape parameter can be obtained through previous studies of similar products and engineering experience in practice. Thus, this study proposes two novel parameter estimation methods, the three-step MLE method and the hybrid estimation method, to address the problems of obtaining the priors, large bias, and inconsistency of previous methods under heavily censored data. For the three-step MLE method, the initial estimates of shape and scale parameters are first derived using MLE, then are updated by the single parameter MLE method with the range constraint of shape parameter. For the hybrid estimation method, the shape parameter is estimated by LSE with the existing range constraint of shape parameter, then the scale parameter estimate can be obtained by MLE. Besides, two numerical examples are carried out to demonstrate the consistency and effectiveness of the proposed methods, the sensitivity analysis results show that the range of shape parameter and the effective sample size have an important effect on the estimation accuracy of parameters. Finally, a case study on turbine engines is given to verify the applicability of the proposed methods, and the comparison results with the traditional methods show the effectiveness of the proposed methods.

The presented research provides two parameter estimation approaches for Weibull distribution under the heavily Type-II censored data. In future studies, other common life distributions should be studied, such as the two-parameter Exponential distribution and Lognormal distribution. Furthermore, the proposed methods can be extended to other common censoring schemes, such as the Type-I and hybrid censoring schemes. Finally, the proposed methods at present are applied to the point estimates of parameters, which is necessary to get accurate interval estimates of distribution parameters and reliability indexes for the heavily censored data.
