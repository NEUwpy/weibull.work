# Grey-based approach for estimating Weibull model and its application

Xiaomei Liu<sup>a,b</sup> and Naiming Xie<sup>a</sup>

<sup>a</sup>College of Economics and Management, Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics, Nanjing, Jiangsu Province, People’s Republic of China; <sup>b</sup>College of Science, Jiujiang University, Jiujiang, Jiangxi Province, People’s Republic of China

## ABSTRACT

Weibull distribution is widely used in engineering because of its flexibility to take on many different shapes. For different application fields, people put forward different estimation methods, including grey estimation method. The purpose of this paper is to improve the existing grey estimation method of Weibull distribution as its some serious shortcomings. Firstly, a grey three-parameter Weibull model is proposed in a discrete form. Then, by means of the grey Weibull model, a two-stage hybrid method for estimating the three-parameter Weibull distribution is proposed, which is composed of the linear and nonlinear least square principles. To demonstrate the feasibility of the proposed grey Weibull model, a simulation study was conducted and compared the results with the modified MLE method, and a comparison study on the frequency analysis of four breakdown data sets of epoxy resins is also carried out. Moreover, as a practical application, Weibull model based on the grey estimation approach is applied to the frequency analysis of monthly wind speed of Hohhot in 2018, and compared with a series of existing estimation methods. Results show that the proposed grey Weibull estimation improves the existing grey estimation and can be well applied to the frequency analysis of Weibull data.

## 1. Introduction

Weibull distribution is widely used in engineering technology because of its flexibility to take on many different shapes, such as survival analysis, reliability and failure analysis, extreme risk analysis and frequency analysis. For different types of data modeling, various estimation methods of Weibull distribution have been studied in the literature. However, in practical applications, only the two-parameter Weibull distribution is easy to estimate and more widely used, while the Weibull model with three or more parameters is less used because of the complexity of the estimation. Therefore, it is meaningful to estimate various Weibull models with specific system modeling mechanism.

There is no consensus on the estimation of three-parameter Weibull distribution. In addition to the common maximum likelihood estimation (MLE), many different methods are developed for different applications. Hirose (1991) proposed an extended MLE with complete unexamined failure samples. Qiao and Tsokos (1995) applied an algorithm to obtain a good estimation. Abbasi, Rabelo, and Hosseinkouchack (2008) used an artificial neural network method to estimate three parameters at the same time. Kundu and Raqab (2009) discussed the estimation of stress intensity parameters for the independent three-parameter Weibull distributions with the same shape and location parameters but different proportional parameters. Cousineau (2009a) pointed out the hybrid estimation method is better than the weighted MLE and the common MLE method for small sample size; and also showed how to calculate weights to eliminate the bias contained in the maximum likelihood estimation equation (Cousineau 2009b). Sur€ uc€ u and Sazak (€ 2009) investigated a robust estimation technology. Markovi, Juki, and Beni (2009) combined the nonparametric method and parametric method; and also considered the nonlinear weighted least squares method (Markovic and Jukic 2010). Abbasi et al. (2011) presented a new hybrid method based on the variable neighborhood search and simulated annealing to maximize the likelihood function. Nagatsuka, Balakrishnan, and Kamakura (2013) constructed a data transformation method to propose a new parameter estimation. Moeini et al. (2013) developed cross entropy method for the MLE. Harper, Eschenbach, and James (2011) evaluated 12 statistical data pack ages of four real world data sets, and analyzed the different methods between statistical data packages. Akram and Hayat (2014) compared the small sample performance for various estimations in terms of bias and root mean squared error. Nagatsuka and Balakrishnan (2015) proposed an effective method for estimating the parameters and quantiles to avoid the problem of unbounded likelihood by using the statistical information of unknown position invariance. Orkc<sup>€</sup> u, Aksoy, and Dog€ -an (2015) presented an effective approach based on the differential evolution algorithm operators for the likelihood function to enhance the estimation accuracy. Park (2017) proposed a sample correlation coefficient estimation method. Montoya, Dıaz-Frances, and Gudelia (2019) proposed a correct likelihood for the three-parameter Weibull case by taking into account the occurrence of the smallest observation with respect to the threshold param eter. Garrido et al. (2019) analyzed six existing estimations of Weibull distribution to show how the various estimators of Weibull model work in a wide range of sample sizes, and also proposed a new method of fitting Weibull distribution using neural network. Yang, Ren, and Hu (2019) applied an evolutionary strategy to explore a good solution by transforming the maximization process of likelihood function into an opti mization problem. To sum up, the statistics estimation methods of the three-parameter Weibull distribution have highly variability between different samples, and the estimation accuracy is generally low in the case of small samples. In view of these, some Chinese scholars introduced grey system theory to estimate the three-parameter Weibull distribution.

Grey system theory, like probability statistics, fuzzy mathematics and rough set theory, is an approach for studying uncertain systems. It was first proposed by Chinese scholar professor Deng (1982). Up to now, grey system theory has produced more effectiveness by combining with other approaches (Liu, Yang, and Forrest 2016; Liu and Xie 2019; Su, Xie, and Yang 2021). In 1989, Zheng and Qin (1989) proposed a method called Zheng’s grey Weibull estimation method to estimate the three-parameter Weibull distribution. And since then, Zheng’s method has been applied in practice. Yan et al. (2005) and Shi, Yang, and Chen (2009) compared different parameter estimation methods under complete samples, including Zheng’s grey estimation method. Hao et al. (2017) analyzed the advantages and disadvantages of the correlation coefficient method, the MLE and Zheng’s grey estimation in terms of cumulative distribution function, sample size and data sensitivity. Gao et al. (2018) presented a new estimation based on the combination of grey model and support vector machine for the case of small samples. Wang et al. (2019) used Zheng’s method to estimate the reliability parameters of small sample electrical protection device. However, these existing grey estimations of three-parameter Weibull distribution are all based on the first order grey model (abbreviated as GM (1.1)), which is the simplest case of grey system theory. In detail, GM (1.1) model is a special discrete form of the first order linear differential equation with constant coefficients, and its analytical solution is in the form of exponential function. So it has inherent limitations in application, for example, it is only suitable for the modeling of monotonic data.

Actually, Zheng and Qin (1989) adopts a double logarithm transformation and a linear regression algorithm to estimate the three parameters of Weibull distribution, so the estimation accuracy is always low. Besides, when Zheng’s grey estimation method is used to estimate the two-parameter Weibull distribution, one key variable of GM (1.1) model will be equal to zero, such that the simulation is greatly limited and sometimes even becomes meaningless. It is regrettable that the follow-up studies of Zheng’s grey Weibull estimation have not improved the problem. Therefore, this paper proposes a new grey estimation approach for the three-parameter Weibull distribution in order to improve the problems existed in Zheng’s grey estimation. The main work of this paper lies in the following aspects:

(1) Propose a grey three-parameter Weibull model.

(2) Propose a two-stage hybrid method composed of the linear and nonlinear least square principles to estimate the three-parameter Weibull distribution.

The outline of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews Zheng’s grey Weibull model. Section 3 proposes a new grey three-parameter Weibull model. Section 4 offers a hybrid estimation method for the three-parameter Weibull distribution. Section 5 presents simulation studies conducted for the feasibility of the new grey Weibull estimation method. Section 6 verifies the new method by a comparison study. Section 7 applies the proposed grey Weibull estimation method for the frequency analysis of wind speed. Section 8 makes conclusions.

## 2. Zheng’s grey weibull model

Assume the cumulative distribution function of three-parameter Weibull distribution is given by

$$
F (x; \beta , \eta , \gamma) = 1 - e ^ {- (\frac {x - \gamma}{\eta}) ^ {\beta}}\tag{1}
$$

where b is the shape parameter, g is the scale parameter, c is the location parameter, and $\beta > 0 , \eta > 0 , \gamma < x$

The corresponding probability density function is

$$
f (x; \beta , \eta , \gamma) = \frac {\beta}{\eta} \left(\frac {x - \gamma}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta - 1} e ^ {- (\frac {x - \gamma}{\eta}) ^ {\beta}}\tag{2}
$$

for $\beta > 0 , \eta > 0 , \gamma < x .$

To describe Zheng’s grey Weibull model, GM (1.1) model is reviewed first here. GM (1.1) model is a difference equation whose continuous form corresponds to an exponential function. The specific definition is as follows:

Definition 1 (Liu, Yang, and Forrest 2016). Assume $X ^ { ( 0 ) } = ( x ^ { ( 0 ) } ( 1 ) , x ^ { ( 0 ) } ( 2 ) , . . . , x ^ { ( 0 ) } ( n ) )$ is an nonnegative sequence, and $X ^ { ( 1 ) } = ( x ^ { ( 1 ) } ( 1 ) , x ^ { ( 1 ) } ( 2 ) , . . . , x ^ { ( 1 ) } ( n ) )$ is the first order accumulating generated operator sequence of $X ^ { ( 0 ) }$ , where $\begin{array} { r } { x ^ { ( 1 ) } ( k ) = \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { k } x ^ { ( 0 ) } ( i ) , k = } \end{array}$ $1 , 2 , . . . , n .$ : Then

$$
x ^ {(0)} (k) + a x ^ {(1)} (k) = b\tag{3}
$$

is referred to as the original form of GM (1.1) model. Here, a is called the development coefficient of GM (1.1) model, b is called the grey input of GM (1.1) model.

The continuous differential equation form corresponding to GM (1.1) model is expressed as

$$
\frac {d x ^ {(1)} (t)}{\mathrm{d} t} + a x ^ {(1)} (t) = b\tag{4}
$$

Assume the initial condition is $\hat { x } ^ { ( 1 ) } ( 1 ) = x ^ { ( 0 ) } ( 1 )$ , then the analytic solution of GM(1,1) model is given by

$$
\hat {x} ^ {(1)} (t) = \frac {b}{a} + \left(x ^ {(0)} (1) - \frac {b}{a}\right) e ^ {- a} \cdot e ^ {- a t}\tag{5}
$$

By means of Equation (5), Zheng presented a grey estimation for the three-parameter Weibull distribution with a variable transformation and a linear regression algorithm. The specific algorithm is as follows:

First, taking the double logarithm transform on both sides of Equation (1) to obtain

$$
\ln \left(- \ln (1 - F (x))\right) = \beta \ln \left(\frac {x - \gamma}{\eta}\right)\tag{6}
$$

And, let

$$
t = - \ln \left(- \ln (1 - F (x))\right)\tag{7}
$$

Then, according to Equation (7), x can be expressed by

$$
x = \gamma + \eta e ^ {- \frac {t}{\beta}}\tag{8}
$$

In this way, Equation (8) becomes an expression in the same form as Equation (5), that is, the analytic solution of GM (1.1) model.

Thus, taking Equation (8) to compare with Equation (5), the parameters of three-parameter Weibull distribution can be estimated by solving the GM (1.1) model using the following formulas

$$
\beta = \frac {1}{a}, \eta = \left(x ^ {(0)} (1) - \frac {b}{a}\right) e ^ {- a}, \gamma = \frac {b}{a},\tag{9}
$$

As GM (1.1) model is solved by a linear least square principle, Zheng’s grey estimation of three-parameter Weibull distribution is similar to the Graphical method. It is known that the fitting accuracy of Graphical method is not high. Moreover, before the implementation of the linear least square principle, a variable transformation is operated during Zheng’s grey estimation. Due to the inevitable increase of some errors in the process of variable transformation, the fitting accuracy of Zheng’s grey estimation may be lower, or even lower than that of Graphical estimation. The case study later in this paper confirms this point.

Besides, the two-parameter Weibull distribution is obtained by setting $\gamma = 0 ;$ , and will cause the grey input b of GM (1.1) model to be 0 constant. That way, GM (1.1) model would be greatly limited in simulating and even sometimes becomes meaningless. Therefore, Zheng’s grey Weibull estimation method may not be suitable for the twoparameter Weibull distribution.

To sum up, it is necessary and meaningful to improve these problems existed in Zheng’s grey Weibull estimation.

## 3. New grey Weibull model

As introduced before, the estimation accuracy of the three-parameter Weibull distribution is generally low in the case of small samples. For most frequency analysis, the number of groups is generally not too large $\left( n < 3 0 \right)$ , so the frequency analysis of three-parameter Weibull data is actual an estimation problem of small sample size. It is noted that, Inoue and Yamada (2006), Hsieh, Jeng, and Kvam (2019) proposed to use discrete models to assessment the software reliability, and the results show the discrete models described with difference equations sometimes yield accurate estimates although the sample size of data is small. On the other hand, the problems existing in Zheng’s grey Weibull model are mainly caused by the shortcomings of GM (1.1) model itself. Therefore, to improve the problems of Zheng’s grey estimation method, the first thing to do is to extend the GM (1.1) model.

That is, based on the principle of GM (1.1) modeling framework, the grey three-parameter Weibull model can be obtained by discretizing the differential equation form of three-parameter Weibull distribution.

The differential equation form of three-parameter Weibull distribution is expressed as

$$
\frac {d F (x)}{d x} = \frac {\beta}{\eta} \left(\frac {x - \gamma}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta - 1} (1 - F (x))\tag{10}
$$

According to Equation (10), we presented a new grey three-parameter Weibull model as follows:

Definition 2. Assume $X ^ { ( 0 ) } = ( x ^ { ( 0 ) } ( t _ { 1 } ) , x ^ { ( 0 ) } ( t _ { 2 } ) , . . . , x ^ { ( 0 ) } ( t _ { n } ) )$ is an nonnegative sequence, here $t _ { 1 } < t _ { 2 } < \cdots < t _ { n } ,$ , and $X ^ { ( 1 ) } = ( x ^ { ( 1 ) } ( t _ { 1 } ) , x ^ { ( 1 ) } ( t _ { 2 } ) , . . . , x ^ { ( 1 ) } ( t _ { n } ) )$ is the first order accumulating generated operator sequence of $X ^ { ( 0 ) }$ , where $\begin{array} { r } { x ^ { ( 1 ) } ( t _ { k } ) = \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { k } x ^ { ( 0 ) } ( t _ { i } ) , k = } \end{array}$ $1 , 2 , . . . , n .$ : Let $\theta \in [ 0 , 1 ]$ , then

$$
x ^ {(0)} (t _ {k + 1}) + \frac {\beta}{\eta^ {\beta}} (t _ {k + 1} - t _ {k}) (t _ {k} - \gamma) ^ {\beta - 1} (\theta x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k}) + (1 - \theta) x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k + 1})) = \frac {\beta}{\eta^ {\beta}} (t _ {k + 1} - t _ {k}) (t _ {k} - \gamma) ^ {\beta - 1}\tag{11}
$$

is named as the grey three-parameter Weibull model.

Here, h is called the grey discrete coefficient, $\beta$ is the shape parameter, g is the scale parameter, $\gamma$ is the location parameter, and $\beta > 0 , \eta > 0 , \gamma < t _ { 1 }$

It can be found that Equation (11) is a discrete difference equation form of three-parameter Weibull distribution if $x ( t )$ is continuous with respect to t, $x ( \gamma ) = 0 , 0 \leq$ $x ( t ) \leq 1$ , and $\operatorname* { l i m } _ { t \to + \infty } x ( t ) = 1$ : This discrete form is different from any common difference scheme of the pure mathematical difference theory. It is generated based on a mapping rule of the grey background information (Deng 1982).

It should be pointed out that the proposed grey three-parameter Weibull model is different from the fuzzy Weibull distribution which is established by modeling for the fuzzy set or by setting the parameters as the fuzzy set (Pak, Parham, and Saraj 2013). The grey three-parameter Weibull model here is to treat the system as a grey system, and assumes the raw data does not strictly follow Weibull distribution before being processed.

As $x ^ { ( 1 ) } ( t _ { k } )$ is nonnegative and increasing for $k = 1 , 2 , . . . , n$ : Thus

$$
\theta x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k}) \leq \theta x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k}) + (1 - \theta) x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k + 1}) \leq x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k + 1}), \theta \in [ 0, 1 ].\tag{12}
$$

And $\beta > 0 , \eta > 0 , \gamma < t _ { 1 }$ , then for the grey three-parameter Weibull model, the follow inequality holds.

$$
\begin{array}{c} \frac {\beta}{\eta} \left(\frac {t _ {k} - \gamma}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta - 1} \theta x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k}) \leq \frac {\beta}{\eta} \left(\frac {t _ {k} - \gamma}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta - 1} (\theta x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k}) + (1 - \theta) x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k + 1})) \\ \leq \frac {\beta}{\eta} \left(\frac {t _ {k} - \gamma}{\eta}\right) ^ {\beta - 1} x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k + 1}) \end{array}\tag{13}
$$

Equation (13) indicates that the grey three-parameter Weibull model under $\theta = 0 . 5$ is more prone to the central trend, which is the same principle as the traditional grey models performing a mean transformation on the accumulated data.

However, different models and different training data may lead to different grey discrete coefficients, that is, there is no evidence that the estimation is optimal under the grey discrete coefficient with $\theta = 0 . 5$ : Therefore, the introduction of grey discrete coefficient h in Definition 2 is to optimize the accumulation generation operator in the process of grey modeling, and to obtain better results. So the grey discrete coefficient in this paper is deemed as a unknown parameter and is optimized as a variable in the interval ½0, 1 to search for the best coefficient.

## 4. New grey estimation of three-parameter Weibull distribution

For the grey three-parameter Weibull model, there are four undetermined parameters $\beta , \eta , \gamma , \theta$ which will be estimated by a hybrid method composed of linear and nonlinear least square principle in two stages.

Firstly, substitute the raw data series into Equation (11) to obtain a linear equation system with respect to a, where $\alpha = \beta \eta ^ { - \beta }$

$$
\left\{ \begin{array}{l} x ^ {(0)} (t _ {2}) + \alpha (t _ {2} - t _ {1}) (t _ {1} - \gamma) ^ {\beta - 1} (\theta x ^ {(1)} (t _ {1}) + (1 - \theta) x ^ {(1)} (t _ {2})) = \alpha (t _ {2} - t _ {1}) (t _ {1} - \gamma) ^ {\beta - 1} \\ x ^ {(0)} (t _ {3}) + \alpha (t _ {3} - t _ {2}) (t _ {2} - \gamma) ^ {\beta - 1} (\theta x ^ {(1)} (t _ {2}) + (1 - \theta) x ^ {(1)} (t _ {3})) = \alpha (t _ {3} - t _ {2}) (t _ {2} - \gamma) ^ {\beta - 1} \\ \dots \\ x ^ {(0)} (t _ {n}) + \alpha (t _ {n} - t _ {n - 1}) (t _ {n - 1} - \gamma) ^ {\beta - 1} (\theta x ^ {(1)} (t _ {n - 1}) + (1 - \theta) x ^ {(1)} (t _ {n})) = \alpha (t _ {n} - t _ {n - 1}) (t _ {n - 1} - \gamma) ^ {\beta - 1} \end{array} \right.\tag{14}
$$

Solving the linear equation system above according to the least square principle to obtain the symbolic solution of a:

$$
\alpha = \frac {\sum_ {k = 2} ^ {n} x ^ {(0)} (t _ {k}) (t _ {k - 1} - \gamma) ^ {\beta} (1 - \theta x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k - 1}) - (1 - \theta) x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k})) / (t _ {k} - t _ {k - 1})}{\sum_ {k = 2} ^ {n} \{(t _ {k - 1} - \gamma) ^ {\beta} (1 - \theta x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k - 1}) - (1 - \theta) x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k})) \} ^ {2}}\tag{15}
$$

Such that g is represented with $\beta , \gamma , \theta _ { : }$ , and $\beta , \gamma , \theta$ would be optimized by:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \text {Min} \sum_ {k = 1} ^ {n} (\hat {x} (t _ {k}) - x (t _ {k})) ^ {2} \\ \left\{ \begin{array}{l} \hat {x} (t _ {k}) = 1 - e ^ {- (\frac {t _ {k} - \gamma}{\eta}) ^ {\beta}} \\ \alpha = \frac {\sum_ {k = 2} ^ {n} x ^ {(0)} (t _ {k}) (t _ {k - 1} - \gamma) ^ {\beta} (1 - \theta x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k - 1}) - (1 - \theta) x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k})) / (t _ {k} - t _ {k - 1})}{\sum_ {k = 2} ^ {n} \{(t _ {k - 1} - \gamma) ^ {\beta} (1 - \theta x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k - 1}) - (1 - \theta) x ^ {(1)} (t _ {k})) \} ^ {2}} \\ 0 \leq \theta \leq 1, 0 <   \beta <   1 0, 0 \leq \gamma <   t _ {1} \end{array} \right. \end{array}\tag{16}
$$

The computational steps of the new grey estimation method of three-parameter Weibull distribution can be summarized as follows:

Step 1: Calculate the corresponding cumulative sequence $\{ x ^ { ( 1 ) } ( t _ { n } ) \}$ based on the observed data sequence $\{ x ^ { ( 0 ) } ( t _ { n } ) \}$

Step 2: Substitute $\{ x ^ { ( 0 ) } ( t _ { n } ) \}$ and $\{ x ^ { ( 1 ) } ( t _ { n } ) \}$ into Equation (15) to obtain the symbolic solution of a with $\beta , \gamma , \theta$

Step 3: Substitute the symbolic solution of <sub>a</sub> into Equation (16) to obtain the optimal values of $\theta , \beta , \gamma$ according to the nonlinear least square principle.

Step 4: Return to obtain the optimal value of <sub>a</sub> and <sub>g</sub> using $\beta , \gamma , \theta$ estimated in Step 3.

Step 5: Substitute the values of $\beta , \eta , \gamma$ into the three-parameter Weibull distribution to simulate the observed data and to do error analysis.

The nonlinear optimization in Step 3 needs to be done by some computer software. In this paper, the optimization is realized using the genetic algorithm (GA) by programming in MATLAB, and the GA code of MATLAB of one run is shown in Appendix.

## 5. Simulation study

## 5<sub>.</sub>1<sub>.</sub> Data generation process

In this section, a simulation study was used to analysis the performance of the developed grey estimation method to estimate the three-parameter Weibull distribution. We consider two cases with the given parameters. The true parameter values are taken as $\beta = 2 , \eta = 2 , \gamma = 2$ in Case 1 and $\beta = 2 , \eta = 3 , \gamma = 5$ in Case 2, where $\beta$ is the shape parameter, g is the scale parameter and $\gamma$ is the location parameter. In both cases, based on three different sample sizes $n = 2 0 , n = 5 0$ and $n = 1 0 0$ , we generated 500 samples in frequency distribution format to estimate the input variables, respectively. For each distribution we use the developed grey estimation method and the modified MLE method to estimate Weibull parameters, thus, we obtain 500 estimated values of each parameter of each approach. And in each run, the parameter estimation error is measured by the absolute percentage error criterion:

$$
A P E = \left| \frac {\hat {\theta} - \theta}{\theta} \right| \times 100 \%
$$

where $\hat { \boldsymbol { \theta } }$ is the estimated value of a parameter $\theta .$

## 5 2 Results of the simulation

For each estimated parameter, we report the sample mean, sample standard derivation and the average of APEs for 500 replications in Tables 1 and 2. Meanwhile, for each approach we plot the boxplots of APEs of each sample size for 500 replications in Figures 1–3, so as to make the comparison of the estimation accuracy among the two estimation methods intuitively.

In the following, we compare the developed grey estimation and the modified MLE methods from these parameter estimates.

In both cases, the estimated parameters in Tables 1 and 2 share some similarities: with sample size increasing, parameters have smaller sample standard derivations and the average of APEs, especially for the modified MLE, which indicates that the MLE is more sensitive to the sample size; For each parameter, the sample mean converges to real values as the sample size increases, which shows that increasing the sample size raises the accuracy of estimation, which leads to more accuracy being obtained in the estimations of the input variables.

Meanwhile, the simulation results in the two cases also show some differences. The averages of the APEs and sample standard derivations of each parameter estimated by the modified MLE are greater than those of each parameter estimated by the proposed grey estimation. Therefore, the proposed grey estimation performs well than the modified MLE method when the parameters and the sample size are all small.

Similarly, Figures 1–3 shows that the APEs offered by the modified MLE has more outliers and greater dispersion for all sample size in both cases. Moreover, with the increase of sample size, the APEs obtained by the proposed grey estimation becomes centralized, while the APEs of the modified MLE still shows large dispersion. That is, whatever size the sample is, the proposed grey estimation has significant superior performance than the modified MLE when using frequency data to estimate parameters. Therefore, when using frequency data to estimate parameters, the proposed grey estimation outperforms the modified MLE, especially in terms of robustness, due to that the grey estimation method takes full use of modeling samples.

## 6. Comparison study

In this Section, a comparison study is conducted to test the effectiveness of the new grey three-parameter Weibull distribution estimation method. The numerical experiment is to use the three-parameter Weibull distribution to simulate four frequency data of electrical breakdown of epoxy resins which come from Hirose (1996). The estimation error is measured by the root mean square error criterion:

$$
R M S E = \sqrt {\frac {1}{n} \sum_ {k = 1} ^ {n} \left(\hat {x} (t _ {k}) - x (t _ {k})\right) ^ {2}}
$$

where $\hat { { \boldsymbol x } } \big ( t _ { k } \big )$ is the estimated value of actual frequencyxðt<sub>k</sub>Þ of group interval $\left( t _ { k } , t _ { k + 1 } \right]$

All results are listed in Table 4.

Table 3 implies that the location parameters of three-parameter Weibull distribution should be smaller than 25. From Table 4, it can be seen that parameters estimated by the extended MLE and the new grey estimation method are all in normal ranges. However, most of the parameters estimated by Zheng’s grey estimation method are abnormal, not only the values of location parameters exceed 25, but also some parameters are negative. Besides, the extended MLE and the new grey estimation all have the root mean square error (RMSE) of nearly 0.02, but the RMSEs of Zheng’s method are all bigger than 0.23. In data 1 and 4, the RMSEs for the extended MLE and the proposed method are close. However, in data 2, the RMSE of the proposed method (RMSE 0.0265) is much smaller than that of the extended MLE (RMSE 0.0890).

The extend MLE method is proposed by Hirose (1991) and the limitations of the extended method is also pointed out by Hirose (1996). He found that some data sets have infinite MLE of parameters when it was attempted to obtain the MLE of the 3-parameter Weibull distribution for the small sample size data. That is, when the extend MLE method is used to estimate the three parameter Weibull distribution, the problem of parameter divergence sometimes occurs when the sample size is small. Considering that some experiments are very expensive, so such a large number of test pieces is unusual. Therefore, the proposed grey estimation method can be an alternative method of the extend MLE method.

## 8. Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a new grey estimation for the three-parameter Weibull distribution in order to improve the existing grey estimation method. Aiming at the limitation of simulation existing in Zheng’s grey estimation of the two-parameter Weibull distribution, which is caused by one zero key variable of GM (1.1) model, this paper proposes a new grey Weibull model to solve this problem, and not only can well estimate the two-parameter Weibull, but also the three-parameter Weibull. Furthermore, to improve the shortcoming of single linear least square algorithm in the optimization process of Zheng’s grey estimation of three-parameter Weibull, a hybrid estimation method combining the linear and the nonlinear least square principle is presented. Results of simulation study reveal that the proposed grey estimation has significant superior performance than the modified MLE when using frequency data to estimate parameters. And results of comparison study in fitting four failure data of electrical breakdown also shows that the new grey three-parameter Weibull distribution estimation method has a higher accuracy than Zheng’s grey estimation and the extended MLE. Besides, the case study for simulating 12 monthly frequency data of wind speed confirms that the new grey estimation method performance better than Zheng’s grey estimation and those conventional statistical methods, including the Graphical, the Moment and the modified MLE.
